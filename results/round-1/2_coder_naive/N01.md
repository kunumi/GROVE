## Guidelines for Pinning Down a Feature's Requirements with Domain Experts Before Coding

---

### 1. Recognise ambiguity as the primary enemy — it must be actively resolved
Real-world software projects suffer from two primary requirements-related problems: incompleteness and **[SWEBOK excerpt b5c5fc05c75acb2e432f]." Treat the loose prose as a warning sign and do not proceed to code until the single correct interpretation is established.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 6bba11b28d752c35061f]"*)

---

### 2. Bring all relevant stakeholder classes into the conversation
Identify and involve all stakeholder classes — clients, customers, users, **subject matter experts (SMEs)**, operations staff, and developers. Doing stakeholder analysis "[SWEBOK excerpt 420f1338de9d7a68c5ce]."

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 034b616ad38b62096b20]"*)

---

### 3. Treat elicitation as an active, not passive, process
"[SWEBOK excerpt 794a6ba38ecb16fcd318]."

Probe for unstated assumptions and hidden knowledge rather than simply transcribing what domain experts volunteer.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 11007c66078e017f31d4]"*)

---

### 4. Hold facilitated workshops (JAD/JRP) to reach shared understanding together
Common elicitation techniques include "joint application development (JAD), joint requirements planning (JRP) and other facilitated workshops." These collaborative sessions gather all parties — business and technical — in the same room to surface and reconcile differing interpretations before work begins.

(source: *"joint application development (JAD), joint requirements planning (JRP) and other facilitated workshops"*)

---

### 5. Use the 5-Whys to reach the true problem, not a presumed solution
"[SWEBOK excerpt 0433e09eb2734db3ed23] [SWEBOK excerpt 716db07833277b41892e].'"

(source: *"The 5-whys technique [SWEBOK excerpt 20f776590023e6560f73]"*)

---

### 6. Write each requirement so it has exactly one interpretation, is testable, and is atomic
[SWEBOK excerpt ad1b579ca555165f2b76]"**
- **"atomic, represent a single decision"**

If a prose statement cannot satisfy all three properties, rewrite it until it does.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 3f9aa2c4f8f16bd73d76]; atomic, represent a single decision"*)

---

### 7. Switch from free prose to structured natural language
"[SWEBOK excerpt 4eecb83f896611f6153d]." For example, using the **actor-action format** ("[SWEBOK excerpt 4214c528e5ef7b2dccea]'") makes actor, trigger, action, and condition explicit and unambiguous.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 4eecb83f896611f6153d]"*)

---

### 8. Write acceptance criteria using ATDD/BDD scenarios before any code is written
In ATDD/BDD, engineers, **business domain experts**, and QA professionals "meet before any production design or [SWEBOK excerpt acf2737e4c17b2e47a7b]." The BDD "Given / When / Then" form is concrete enough that domain experts can read and confirm it, while being precise enough for developers. "[SWEBOK excerpt 8c635b52f6833b7f34d2]."

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 8c635b52f6833b7f34d2]"*)

---

### 9. Supplement text with models when further precision is needed
"[SWEBOK excerpt 0a901e1b1ef4bc1d2ecd]." The more formal the model, the less ambiguous it is.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt f0faa050fcaa6a3320af])"*)

### 11. Use prototypes to surface and correct misinterpretations
"[SWEBOK excerpt a45aecf97f714005b1c9]."

### Summary of the workflow
> Identify all stakeholders → hold a facilitated workshop → actively probe for tacit knowledge → apply 5-Whys to find the true need → rewrite the feature as structured, atomic, testable statements → agree on BDD/ATDD Given-When-Then scenarios with domain experts → validate through multi-perspective review (and optionally a prototype) → only then write code.
