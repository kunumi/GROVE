## Guidelines for Choosing Between Candidate Libraries Using Mixed Monetary and Non-Monetary Criteria

---

### 1. Frame the problem as a multiple-attribute decision from the start

Money is usually the most important single criterion, but it is not always the only one. Explicitly acknowledge that performance, maturity, community activity, and team familiarity are legitimate criteria alongside license cost, so the right decision method is selected from the outset.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt b4a03fe3cebc8941c34c]."*)

---

### 2. Define and limit your selection criteria deliberately

Too many criteria make the analysis unwieldy; too few may fail to differentiate the candidates. Choose a manageable set that meaningfully distinguishes the three libraries.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt ad779e7ae7316b9713b2]."*)

---

### 3. Balance breadth of criteria against the cost of analysing them

Adding more criteria improves the decision only up to the point where the extra analysis effort is justified.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 6e92682341c43c595e4b]."*)

---

### 4. Express every criterion as objectively as possible, and explicitly label what cannot be quantified

Aim for objective, ideally monetary, expressions. For criteria such as community activity or team familiarity that resist monetisation, label them as intangibles and treat them explicitly rather than ignoring them.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 4b188f0e49c58e432250]. [SWEBOK excerpt a4318de5004b25042ec1].'"*)

---

### 5. Evaluate all alternatives against each criterion on a consistent basis

When comparing licence cost, use the same planning horizon and the same kinds of costs for all three libraries. Extending the time frame for one candidate but not others will artificially favour the shorter view.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 52782f508d83e0a6b786] … [SWEBOK excerpt 8c6be2eafa754bc08485]."*)

---

### 6. Apply a compensatory technique (such as additive weighting or AHP) to combine monetary and non-monetary scores into a single figure of merit

Compensatory techniques collapse all criteria — including non-monetary ones — into a single comparable score. For any given library, a weaker score on one criterion can be offset by stronger scores on others.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt dd90612de75ae5a7ee4c])."*)

---

### 7. Consider non-compensatory techniques as a cross-check or when certain criteria are absolute

If a library fails a minimum threshold on a critical criterion (e.g., the license is legally incompatible), non-compensatory methods such as dominance, satisficing, or lexicography let you eliminate it without allowing any other score to rescue it.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt ceccdfe5823e12c32f0a]."*)

---

### 8. Explicitly account for intangible organisational assets — including team knowledge

The team's existing familiarity with a library is an intangible knowledge asset. Failing to factor it in risks choosing a solution that does not fit the organisation's real capabilities.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 5eed27bb7bc8930d99e2] … [SWEBOK excerpt 09b7160aaa0f99ea2a58]."*)

---

### 9. Establish the trade-off goals first, and conduct the analysis objectively and impartially

Before scoring the libraries, agree on and document what each criterion means and how important it is. Then apply those definitions consistently, and disclose any conflict of interest.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 3a11c63166fee03d5486] [SWEBOK excerpt 70b849353f396ebaa2db] … [SWEBOK excerpt 00f7fbea150e1817bf17]."*)

---

### 10. Perform sensitivity analysis and consider ranges of estimates to test how robust the choice is

Scores for maturity or performance may be uncertain. If changing an estimate by a plausible amount would flip the decision, that inaccuracy must be resolved before committing.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 820c1d2bbc4d8e1e6c9d]. [SWEBOK excerpt cc452cd27b746130520e] [SWEBOK excerpt 4e41c08a45517283af71]."*)

---

### 11. Apply structured risk-reasoning when outcome probabilities can (or cannot) be estimated

If you can assign probabilities to risks such as a library becoming unsupported, use expected-value or decision-tree techniques. If you cannot, apply uncertainty rules such as the Minimax Regret Rule.

> (source: *"Decision-making-under-risk techniques [SWEBOK excerpt 755450bbbfe983b724e1] … Monte Carlo analysis, decision trees … decision-making-under-uncertainty techniques [SWEBOK excerpt da0eb750169744453b8c]."*)

---

### 12. Document the chosen alternative and the rationale for it

For a decision with real consequences, record which library was selected and why, so the reasoning can be reviewed, challenged, and improved if conditions change.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 9a58be7e9faa975f6362]."*)

---

### 13. Plan to validate your estimates after selection by monitoring actual performance

Because the decision rests on estimates (of performance, maturity trajectory, learning curve, etc.), close the loop by comparing those estimates to what actually occurs. This improves future decisions.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 0a22155f16372db877ef]. This also helps improve estimation over time."*)

---

### 14. Verify that the acquired library is complete, correct, and fit for its intended environment regardless of which is chosen

Regardless of the selection outcome, validate that the chosen library integrates correctly with your architecture and satisfies its requirements in its actual operating environment.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt f19cb16ebb54a4c90c13] … [SWEBOK excerpt 1eb65fd9a124cf38c3d2]."*)
