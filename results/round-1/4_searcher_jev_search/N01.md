## Guidelines for Making a Feature Requirement Unambiguous

---

### 1. Recognise ambiguity as the core problem you are solving

Understand that loose prose is dangerous by design: natural language is inherently ambiguous, and requirements written that way are "[SWEBOK excerpt 6fe2fd3c9e0a7f925190]." Make removing that ambiguity the explicit goal of every activity listed below.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 6bba11b28d752c35061f]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Software Requirements Engineering Fundamentals/Requirements Problems in Software Projects.md`)

---

### 2. Apply the "5-whys" to surface the true underlying problem

Before writing a single formal sentence, repeatedly ask "Why is this the requirement?" until everyone agrees that stopping earlier would leave the stakeholder's real problem unsolved. This prevents the team from specifying a *solution* instead of a *need*, which is a frequent source of misreading.

(source: "The 5-whys technique … [SWEBOK excerpt 716db07833277b41892e].'" — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md`)

---

### 3. Elicit using facilitated workshops with domain experts

Replace informal prose discussions with structured joint sessions. Techniques such as **Joint Application Development (JAD)**, **joint requirements planning (JRP)**, facilitated workshops, interviews, focus groups, and **user-story mapping** bring domain experts and engineers into the same room to surface tacit knowledge and reach shared understanding before specification begins.

(source: "joint application development (JAD), joint requirements planning (JRP) and other facilitated workshops … [SWEBOK excerpt 794a6ba38ecb16fcd318]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Stakeholder Identification and Requirements Elicitation Techniques/Stakeholder Elicitation Techniques and Requirements Sources.md`)

---

### 4. Write every requirement so that it satisfies the individual quality criteria

Rewrite the loose prose into discrete statements that are each:
- **Unambiguous** — interpretable in only one way;
- **Testable (quantified)** — compliance or non-compliance can be clearly demonstrated;
- **Atomic** — represents a single decision;
- **Written in stakeholder vocabulary** — uses the domain experts' own words;
- **Acceptable to all stakeholders** — no one dissents.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 6eabf6d17bfa31257476]; … atomic, represent a single decision; … [SWEBOK excerpt 825d8b93845096610f26]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md`)

---

### 5. Ensure the full set of requirements is complete, consistent, and covers exceptions

The collection of requirements as a whole must be:
- **Complete** — boundary conditions, exception conditions, and security needs are all addressed;
- **[SWEBOK excerpt d3b56609c4e4e49d121a] source material (e.g., the original feature request).

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 5de84dd14499925cfd0c]; … [SWEBOK excerpt 455f3e88227d89ca2ce5]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md`)

---

### 6. Replace prose with structured natural language (actor-action format)

Impose a fixed grammatical template to increase precision. For example, the **actor-action format** specifies: `[triggering event] + actor + action + [condition/qualification]`. An example: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 4214c528e5ef7b2dccea]'."* This format eliminates the ambiguity of unstructured sentences.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt c6277fe69265442c6926]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Requirement Specification Formats and Structures/Actor-Action Format in Requirements Statements.md`)

---

### 7. Use user stories plus BDD/ATDD scenarios as the primary specification medium (before any code)

Convene a meeting of **software engineers, business domain experts, and QA professionals before any design or [SWEBOK excerpt fe256e02b2cf0482566d] concrete scenarios in the form:

> *"Given \<context\>, when \<stimulus\>, then \<outcome\>."*

Each scenario is an unambiguous, precise statement of a requirement in test-case language, which "directly addresses the requirements ambiguity problem" because "[SWEBOK excerpt 1f4cdc1ed4b2cc8d57f8]."

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt cd5cc3b984affe631643] [SWEBOK excerpt acf2737e4c17b2e47a7b]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Test-Driven Development and Acceptance Testing/Acceptance Criteria-Based Requirements Specification.md`)

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 8c635b52f6833b7f34d2]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Test-Driven Development and Requirements Specification/Acceptance Criteria and BDD Scenarios for Banking Systems.md`)

---

### 8. Cover all scenarios, including boundary and negative cases

Write BDD/ATDD scenarios not only for the happy path but also for boundary conditions, insufficient data, disabled accounts, and other edge cases. SWEBOK explicitly notes that scenarios for "account has an insufficient balance," "bank card has been disabled," and "ATM does not contain enough money" are all *necessary*. Covering them removes the ambiguity that always lurks at the edges of a feature.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt c3b1fde660533ead3d17] [SWEBOK excerpt a052406d6c679406b706]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Test-Driven Development and Requirements Specification/Acceptance Criteria and BDD Scenarios for Banking Systems.md`)

---

### 9. Add metadata attributes to each requirement for traceability and accountability

Document, alongside each requirement statement: its **rationale** (why it matters), its **source** (which stakeholder imposed it), its **acceptance criteria**, its **dependencies** and **conflicts**, and its **change history**. This metadata makes each requirement's intent explicit and prevents later reinterpretation.

(source: "Possible additional attributes include the following: … [SWEBOK excerpt 59d7cf197b35b182a65d]); … acceptance criteria; … dependencies; conflicts; … the requirement's change history." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Behavioral Models and Requirements Documentation/Formal Requirements Models and Additional Attributes.md`)

---

### 10. Validate by multi-perspective review before freezing the specification

Conduct a **requirements review** in which three distinct reviewer groups each inspect the written requirements from their own viewpoint:
1. **Clients, customers, and users** — confirm their needs are completely and accurately represented;
2. **Requirements-specialist engineers** — confirm the document is clear and conforms to standards;
3. **Architects and developers** — confirm [SWEBOK excerpt 28ec65f9a9aa744ceca0].

Provide reviewers with a **checklist or "definition of done"** to keep them focused on specific quality aspects.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt a36fb4be0a88137baf40] … [SWEBOK excerpt d78de3b847c261741b2b]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Requirements Validation and Management/Requirements Validation Methods and Techniques.md`)

---

### 11. Simulate or "execute" the specification with non-technical stakeholders

If domain experts are unlikely to work through a written document carefully, produce a **model-based specification or prototype** that can be hand-interpreted or animated. Walk stakeholders through a sufficient set of demonstration scenarios so they can confirm—or refute—[SWEBOK excerpt 2240dc2d444bfc65fc39]."

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 25555d1d7a1f36132af3]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Requirements Validation and Management/Requirements Validation Methods and Techniques.md`)

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 78d5d553044949c2fea4] … [SWEBOK excerpt 786472a44a8cccda297d]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Requirements Validation and Management/Prototyping for Requirements Validation and Feedback.md`)

---

### 12. Consider formal or model-based notation for the highest-precision requirements

Where the risk of misreading is greatest, supplement prose and BDD scenarios with **UML/SysML models** or formally defined notations. "[SWEBOK excerpt fc0fbdedef0edd495391]." Formal models also enable mechanical analysis to prove the absence of contradictions.
