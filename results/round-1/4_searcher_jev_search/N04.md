## Guidelines for Safer Version Rollouts

### 1. Separate the concepts of "deployment" and "release" — and control them independently

A **deployment** is the installation of a version into an environment; a **release** is the act of making it available to customers. These are distinct decisions. Making a feature available to, say, 5 % of your customer base first is a valid release decision. Keep the two steps separate so that a deployed version can be evaluated before being released to everyone.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 414157672a039a02b9ba])" — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Operations, Reliability, and Security Management/Deployment and Release Engineering in Software Operations.md`)

---

### 2. Use canary release testing to expose a new version to a limited audience first

Deploy the new version partially and for a limited time before committing to a full rollout. The evaluation during this period lets operations engineers decide whether to proceed.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt d8f6ea40078561c9b855]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Automated Deployment and Canary Release Testing.md`)

---

### 3. Watch the newly deployed software automatically before promoting it further

After deployment, automated tooling should observe the software for a period to ensure it does not crash or misbehave before it is promoted to full release.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt b21e029debd98a49de44]" — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Automated Deployment and Canary Release Testing.md`)

---

### 4. Adopt environment-based release strategies (staging environments)

Use a staging environment to test the new version before exposing it to production users. The basic strategy involves deploying to staging first, validating it there, and only then releasing to production.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt a11c7d9230b04a96dbd6]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Release Engineering Strategies and Risk Reduction.md`)

---

### 5. Use application-based release strategies (feature toggles) to control exposure without redeployment

Feature toggles allow you to enable or disable specific sections of code—including new features—using configuration parameters. This decouples deployment from exposure, so a faulty feature can be turned off instantly without reverting the entire version.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 903a8e172cabfed304df]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Release Engineering Strategies and Risk Reduction.md`)

---

### 6. Plan, rehearse, and automate rollback *before* every deployment

A rollback plan must be in place and practiced before the new version goes live — not improvised after a problem is detected. Automated surveillance should be able to trigger rollback and data migration so quickly that users do not notice the problem.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 49c5409c0fdd4e7e5693] [SWEBOK excerpt 38f6b8d3f384481e85f9]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Rollback and Problem Resolution in DevOps.md`)

---

### 7. Ensure rollback covers both the software *and* its database

Rollback is not just a code problem. Both the application and its database must be returnable to a known-good state. Treat rollback and data migration as two sides of the same process.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt b0272b579e14ca5ef12d]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Rollback and Data Migration in Software Deployment.md`)

---

### 8. Automate the entire deployment pipeline to reduce errors and speed up recovery

Manual deployment steps are slow and error-prone. Automating packaging, configuration, server restarts, installation, and smoke testing makes each deployment faster and more consistent — and directly shortens recovery time when things go wrong.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 53b297fb412d295b235c]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Operations, Reliability, and Security Management/Deployment and Release Engineering in Software Operations.md`)

---

### 9. Ship smaller, independent units of change rather than large bundled releases

Releasing one small change at a time — a single feature or bug fix, rather than a large version containing many changes — limits the blast radius of any single fault and makes it far easier to pinpoint the cause of a problem.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 0869ad6ca7e1cda11101]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/DevOps Continuous Delivery and Independent Deployments.md`)

---

### 10. Architect the application to enable those small, independent deployments

Small releases only become feasible when the application is designed to allow them. If the system is a monolith tightly coupled across all features, independent deployment of individual changes will remain impractical no matter what the process says.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 92872fada22ddbddd871]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/DevOps Continuous Delivery and Independent Deployments.md`)

---

### 11. Apply continuous delivery to assemble release candidates automatically and test at every stage

Continuous delivery ensures that the latest code and configuration are always assembled into a verifiable release candidate. Combined with continuous testing at every stage of the pipeline, it surfaces problems before they reach production users.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 55ec8e4e112a463d548a]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Maintenance and Configuration Management/Software Maintenance, Evolution, and Quality Assurance/Continuous Integration, Delivery, and Software Maintenance/Continuous Delivery and Testing in Software Development.md`)

---

### 12. Treat the goal of problem resolution as minimising disruption, not just fixing the bug

When a faulty version is detected, the operational response should focus on minimising disruption through identification and analysis of the root cause, involving a multidisciplinary team where needed, and using monitoring, logging, and profiling to investigate underlying infrastructure causes.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 6b8b75ba23c9cfaff0fb] … [SWEBOK excerpt 62226a659ffe7a236c35]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Rollback and Problem Resolution in DevOps.md`)
