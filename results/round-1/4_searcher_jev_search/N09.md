## Guidelines for Combining Multiple Criteria to Choose Among Three Candidate Libraries

---

### 1. Frame the decision as a multiple-attribute problem from the outset

Recognise that licence cost alone is insufficient. When criteria other than money exist and cannot all be reduced to a monetary value, the decision must be treated as a **multiple-attribute decision** that explicitly factors in nonmonetary criteria. Restricting yourself to a single monetary criterion when other attributes matter will lead to a suboptimal choice.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt a29c4eeacc2005294c93] … [SWEBOK excerpt b4a03fe3cebc8941c34c]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Multiple-Attribute Decision-Making Beyond Financial Criteria.md)

---

### 2. Enumerate all reasonable technically feasible candidates before evaluating any of them

Do not start scoring until you are confident the actual best option is among those being considered. If the best solution is never placed in the candidate set, it can never be chosen.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 705a766f02a6e8c3d425]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Evaluating Candidates and Managing Decision Costs/Solution Generation and Feasibility Verification.md)

---

### 3. Define selection criteria explicitly—and calibrate their number carefully

Write down each criterion before scoring. Too many criteria make the analysis unwieldy; too few may fail to differentiate the candidates, leading to a suboptimal choice. Aim for objective expression of each criterion where possible, even when a monetary value cannot be attached.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt ad779e7ae7316b9713b2]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multiple-Attribute Decision Criteria and Evaluation.md)

---

### 4. Label non-price criteria as intangibles and handle them explicitly rather than ignoring them

Performance, maturity, community activity, and team familiarity are **intangibles** (also called "unquantifiables" or "irreducibles"). They affect outcomes but have no direct price tag. They must be identified, characterised, and included in the decision rather than left out because they are hard to price.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt a4318de5004b25042ec1].'" — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multiple-Attribute Decision Criteria and Evaluation.md)

---

### 5. Evaluate every candidate against every criterion on the same basis and over the same planning horizon

When comparing cost or any other criterion, use the same basis of comparison (e.g., present worth over the same time frame) for all three libraries. Using a longer time horizon for one candidate than another will artificially favour whichever uses the shorter horizon.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 52782f508d83e0a6b786] … [SWEBOK excerpt 8c6be2eafa754bc08485]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Establishing Criteria and Evaluation Methods/Evaluating Alternatives Against Selection Criteria.md)

---

### 6. Balance the breadth of criteria against the cost of analysing them

Adding more criteria improves the probability of a good decision but also increases decision-making effort. The potential gain from including an additional criterion must be weighed against the extra analysis work it demands.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 6e92682341c43c595e4b]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Establishing Criteria and Evaluation Methods/Balancing Decision Criteria Against Analysis Effort.md)

---

### 7. Apply a compensatory technique to collapse all criteria into a single score

**Compensatory techniques** (also called single-dimensioned techniques) allow a lower score on one criterion to be offset by a higher score on another. Applicable methods include **nondimensional scaling**, **additive weighting**, and the **Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)**. These are the natural fit when you believe all criteria can legitimately trade off against each other.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt dd90612de75ae5a7ee4c])." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory and Non-Compensatory Decision-Making Techniques.md)

---

### 8. Consider non-compensatory techniques when certain criteria are non-negotiable

If one criterion is a hard threshold (e.g., a library with an unacceptable licence must be eliminated regardless of its other scores), apply **non-compensatory techniques** instead. These include **dominance** (eliminate any alternative that is beaten on all criteria), **satisficing** (eliminate alternatives that fail a minimum threshold), and **lexicography** (rank by the most important criterion first, break ties with the second, and so on). [SWEBOK excerpt 1263b50a1363c80e93f0]; no trade-off is allowed.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt ceccdfe5823e12c32f0a]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory and Non-Compensatory Decision-Making Techniques.md)

---

### 9. Apply all relevant criteria together—there is no simple rule

No single formula automatically produces the right answer. All criteria—business impact, cost, complexity, time, and intangible factors—must be considered together to form a defensible multiple-attribute decision.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 8a8ebbccd3841055e28d] … [SWEBOK excerpt e73b0b3c20d62e93124d]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Intangible Asset Valuation and Software Estimation Methods/Decision-Making for Software Product Selection.md)

---

### 10. Address uncertainty by using ranges of estimates, sensitivity analysis, and delayed final decisions

Because estimates for intangible factors (team ramp-up time, expected community longevity, future performance needs) are imprecise, test whether inaccuracies large enough to change the conclusion exist. If they do, consider deferring the final choice until better data is available.

> (source: "The following techniques [SWEBOK excerpt 4e41c08a45517283af71]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Estimation, Decision Analysis, and Investment Evaluation/Engineering Decision-Making Under Uncertainty and Risk.md)

---

### 11. Record the selected alternative, the rejected alternatives, and the full rationale

Explicitly document which library was chosen, which were rejected, and *why*—the assumptions made, the criteria weights used, and the trade-offs accepted. This prevents repeating poor decisions in the future and allows the team to revisit the choice if conditions change.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 711ea8ffa0096d728dad] … [SWEBOK excerpt 510e33216e1de5586418]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Foundations of Software Architecture/Software Architecture_ Foundations, Views, and Patterns/Architectural Design Languages, Decisions, and Technical Debt/Documenting Architectural Decisions and Their Rationale.md)

---

### 12. Treat third-party library acquisition as a risk-carrying activity requiring explicit controls

Acquiring an external library—regardless of acquisition class (commercial, open-source, etc.)—introduces risks around security, legality, and suitability. These risks must be assessed as part of the selection, not after it, and procedural or technical controls should be introduced where unfiltered access to external repositories is a concern.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 0e0f1116723723eff660] … [SWEBOK excerpt cb6fe94e8957668780a1] … [SWEBOK excerpt b391b67b048a5d6ed3fa]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Quality Management and Project Oversight/Software Acquisition and Project Oversight/Software Acquisition and Supplier Contract Management.md)
