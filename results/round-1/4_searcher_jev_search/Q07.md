## Guidelines for Evaluating Test Suite Fault-Detection Effectiveness Beyond Code Coverage

---

### 1. Use Fault Injection to Measure How Many Seeded Faults Your Tests Reveal

Artificially introduce known faults into the system under test (SUT) before running your suite. The proportion of injected faults discovered tells you how effectively the suite detects defects, and can be used to estimate the number of genuine faults still present.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt b98a01ab11a6bb389235]."

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation.md`)

> **Caveat:** Apply this technique with care—statisticians question how representative injected faults are of real ones, and there is the risk of leaving seeded faults in the SUT. (source: same file)

---

### 2. Compute the Mutation Score to Quantify Test Suite Strength

Apply mutation testing: generate many slightly modified versions (mutants) of the SUT, run your test suite against each one, and measure what fraction of mutants it "kills." A higher mutation score signals stronger fault-detection ability.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt c55903435d9db2cf30b2]."

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation.md`)

### 3. Write New Tests Specifically Targeting Surviving Mutants

Do not stop at measuring the score passively. When mutants survive (are not killed), design new test cases specifically to kill them. This actively closes gaps in fault-detection ability.

### 7. Combine Specification-Based and Structure-Based Techniques

### 8. Apply Reliability Growth Models to Assess Remaining Fault Exposure

Track failures over time and fit reliability growth models to the data. If the failure rate is not decreasing (the model is not "growing"), the suite may not be exercising fault-prone areas effectively; conversely, a levelling-off can support a stop-testing decision.

### 9. Measure Relative Effectiveness by Comparing Techniques Empirically

Do not evaluate a test suite in isolation. Use empirical comparisons—such as the ratio of faults found through testing versus total faults found during and after testing, or [SWEBOK excerpt 28ceef9587ac997f4a32]—to benchmark the suite against alternative techniques or historical baselines.

### 10. Use Operational Profiles to Evaluate Fault Detection Relative to Real-World Usage

Assess whether the test suite exercises the SUT in proportion to how it will actually be used. Faults in high-frequency usage paths matter most; a suite that ignores them cannot be called effective regardless of its other metrics.
