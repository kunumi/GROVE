## Guidelines for Writing Down a Recurring Hand-off Workflow

### A — What the Write-Up Must State

**1. State the required inputs, the transforming activities, and the outputs generated.**

A process description is defined precisely as covering these three elements, and it must acknowledge that the output of one process step can be the input to the next.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 47bd2c965711c92d2c7a] … [SWEBOK excerpt 0abe0273b7db701637cc]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Engineering Processes and System Lifecycles/Software Engineering Process Definition and Structure/Software Process Definition and Components.md`)

---

**2. Specify inputs and outputs at every step, and identify how process steps are interrelated.**

Because life cycle processes depend on each other, the write-up must be explicit about which outputs one step produces and which subsequent step consumes them, capturing the chain of hand-offs.

(source: *"Defining software processes … [SWEBOK excerpt 026d1086dcb5d4d25894]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Processes and Life Cycle Management/Software Process Definition and Life Cycle Specification.md`)

---

**3. Assign and clarify roles and responsibilities for each activity.**

The document must name who does what, so that neither new developers nor automated agents have to guess accountability.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt b673e0e35458e7cbffe6]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/ISO 9001 and Software Quality Assurance Management Systems.md`)

---

**4. Detail the precise steps taken to execute each activity (procedures).**

A high-level description is not enough; individual steps must be spelled out so any practitioner can replicate them without ambiguity.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 9c0494caf24bd8190773]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Quality Management Systems and Process Evaluation/Software Quality Management System Implementation and Evaluation.md`)

---

**5. Include any controls (directives, constraints) and enabling mechanisms (tools, technologies, resources) associated with the process.**

Capturing what is mandatory, what is forbidden, and what tools or infrastructure must be available prevents silent failures at hand-off points.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 8a02b8f495be0b31bbdf]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Engineering Processes and System Lifecycles/Software Engineering Process Definition and Structure/Software Process Definition and Components.md`)

---

**6. Record the rationale for every non-trivial decision in the workflow.**

When people who were not present at the creation of the workflow later modify or maintain it, undocumented rationale is typically lost. Recording it sustains long-term maintainability and allows future practitioners to understand *why*, not only *what*.

(source: *"Work products of software design capture … ([SWEBOK excerpt 718df78c71aa254de924]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Design Concerns, Documentation, and Modeling/Software Design Documentation and Communication Methods/Recording Software Designs.md`)

---

**7. Make the write-up easy to understand and correct, so that it can serve as the basis for coordination, measurement, and quality management.**

The specification should be useful to humans for communication with one another, and easy to understand and correct, because it becomes the foundation for management and improvement activities.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 0b8669ad38df2f57153a]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Processes and Life Cycle Management/Software Process Definition and Life Cycle Specification.md`)

---

**8. Write it [SWEBOK excerpt 22a04305855a928de154] to be verified.**

A process document that ignores its reader fails in practice. The document should explicitly mark where checkpoints occur so deviations can be caught.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 9310ce2116f59aec8e4c] to be verified."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Quality Management Systems and Process Evaluation/Software Quality Management System Implementation and Evaluation.md`)

---

**9. Document relevant facts, significant risks and trade-offs, and warnings of undesirable consequences.**

Good documentation must cover not just the happy path but also what can go wrong and what practitioners must guard against.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 293cdbe4e8c89e3b64f5]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Professional Practice and Collaboration in Software Engineering/Professional Responsibility and Intellectual Property in Software Engineering/Documentation and Requirements Trade-off Management/Documentation Standards and Requirements for Software Engineers.md`)

**10. Elicit tacit knowledge explicitly through interviews, observation, protocol analysis, and task analysis — especially when knowledge currently resides only in a few people's heads.**

Many requirements (and process steps) are tacit; elicitation techniques must be used proactively to surface them before they are written down.

**11. Avoid ambiguity: each statement must be interpretable in only one way.**

Both incompleteness and ambiguity are the primary causes of requirements failure, and the same principle applies to process documentation.

### B — In What Forms Can It Be Written

**12. Plain (unstructured) natural language** — a prose description using statements such as "The developer shall …" This is the simplest form but is inherently ambiguous.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 73fdd84f59c4fe331ecf] … [SWEBOK excerpt a991a1aeecdf14282366] …'"* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Natural Language Requirements Specifications/Unstructured Natural Language Requirements Specification.md`)

---

**13. Structured natural language** — prose constrained to a fixed template (e.g., actor–action format) to increase precision and conciseness while remaining readable.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt c6277fe69265442c6926]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Natural Language Requirements Specifications/Structured Natural Language Requirements Specification.md`)

---

**14. Use case or scenario specifications** — structured templates that capture triggering events, parameters, preconditions (requires), guarantees (postconditions), normal course, alternative courses, and exceptions.

This format directly maps onto a hand-off workflow: each use case names the trigger, who does what, what is assumed going in, and what is guaranteed coming out.

(source: *"A use case specification template … [fields:] Triggering event(s), Parameters, Requires, Guarantees, Normal course, Alternative course(s), Exceptions."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Natural Language Requirements Specifications/Unstructured Natural Language Requirements Specification.md`, Table 24)

---

**15. Graphical process notations** — data-flow diagrams (DFDs), UML activity diagrams, state charts, BPMN diagrams, IDEF0, flowcharts, Petri nets.

These visual forms show the flow of work between people or steps and are particularly readable for hand-off sequences.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 6b3ea1c4bdbe9b51c089]), [SWEBOK excerpt 2a7c513cf4547122d815])."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Process Foundations and Measurement/Software Process Infrastructure Tools and Notations.md`)

---

**16. Formal specification languages** (e.g., Z, VDM, SDL, Event-B) — mathematically grounded languages that allow automated consistency and completeness checking, and that are particularly suitable for automated coding agents because they are unambiguous and mechanically analyzable.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 7c862cc52c6bb02eff73]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Development Methodologies and Approaches/Formal Methods in Software Engineering.md`)

---

**17. Precondition / postcondition / invariant statements** — formal or semi-formal conditions that bound each activity: what must be true before the step starts, what is guaranteed when it ends, and what must remain unchanged throughout.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 60153cf94e6d78567b2b] … [SWEBOK excerpt fb9c517ba81870bbd58a] … [SWEBOK excerpt ee8b9640b384f5fd9a11] … before and after execution."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Semantic Assumptions and Modeling Techniques/Preconditions, Postconditions, and Invariants in Functions.md`)

---

**18. Checklist or forms** — a lightweight but concrete artefact listing mandatory items to be verified at each step or hand-off point.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 7eba0bc486e200efa623] …)."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Quality Assurance Reviews and Implementation Practices/Technical Reviews and Audits in Project Management.md`)

---

**19. Model-based artefacts** (executable models, simulation, MBD) — go beyond static documents by allowing the described process to be animated, simulated, or executed directly, which is especially valuable for automated agents.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt f7e12c7fb38a2e85399f] … [SWEBOK excerpt b12f774cac1143d37da6]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Design Concerns, Documentation, and Modeling/Model-Based Design and Software Notation/Model-Based Design and Development Tools.md`)

**20. The form chosen should match the stakeholder audience and intended use — a single write-up may need multiple representations.**

Different readers (new developers vs. automated agents vs. quality reviewers) need different levels of formality; a single document is unlikely to serve all audiences equally well.
