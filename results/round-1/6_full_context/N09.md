# Guidelines for Choosing Among Three Candidate Libraries

## 1. Recognize this as a multiple-attribute decision, not a purely financial one

When selection criteria beyond money are relevant and cannot all be cast in monetary terms, use multiple-attribute decision-making techniques that allow non-monetary criteria to be factored in alongside cost. Do not reduce the choice to price alone.

(source: Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Multiple-Attribute Decision-Making Beyond Financial Criteria.md)

## 3. Express each criterion as objectively as possible — preferably in monetary terms, but accept non-monetary scales where needed

"[SWEBOK excerpt 4b188f0e49c58e432250]." Criteria that cannot be quantified in money are called unquantifiables or intangibles and must still be included.

## 4. Respect measurement scale types when scoring criteria

Understand whether each criterion is measured on a nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio scale. Scores on an ordinal scale (e.g., "team familiarity: low / medium / high") cannot be arithmetically averaged or multiplied — doing so produces meaningless and potentially misleading results.

(source: Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Measurement Foundations and Scale Types/Ordinal Scales and Their Properties.md)

## 5. Identify and explicitly value the intangible assets at stake

Intangible assets — such as the team's existing know-how, documented processes, and institutional experience — affect the real cost and risk of each option. "[SWEBOK excerpt 79e4188fda81adbe02bc]."

(source: Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Evaluating Complex Proposals and Strategic Alternatives/Do-Nothing Alternative and Intangible Assets in Decision-Making.md)

## 6. Convert the set of options into mutually exclusive alternatives before comparing

Ensure the candidates are framed so that choosing one excludes the others. "[SWEBOK excerpt d64b944a212bb5ec6e62] prevents double-counting benefits or costs.

(source: Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Evaluating Complex Proposals and Strategic Alternatives/Converting Interdependent Proposals into Mutually Exclusive Alternatives.md)

## 7. Include total cost of ownership, not just licence price

The licence cost is only one component. Total cost of ownership (TCO) covers acquisition, activation, and keeping the library running — including integration work, training, ongoing maintenance, and support. "[SWEBOK excerpt e014e10088e20e544d08]."

(source: Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Financial Analysis and Cost Assessment Methods/Total Cost of Ownership in Software Engineering Economics.md)

## 8. Treat community activity and maturity as proxies for maintenance cost and risk

Consider [SWEBOK excerpt 063bcfdba34e4b14e5ba] and technical risk of implementation as explicit scoring factors. A library with low community activity creates higher future maintenance burden and dependency risk, which should be costed or risk-weighted in the analysis.

(source: Requirements Validation and Management/Requirements Management and Prioritization Strategy/Requirement Prioritization Schemes and Factors.md)

## 9. Apply a compensatory technique to [SWEBOK excerpt 6ed292052877d4475a99]

Use additive weighting, non-dimensional scaling, or the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP). These "compensatory techniques" allow a lower score in one criterion to be traded off against a higher score in another, producing a defensible single ranking. "[SWEBOK excerpt db7ebc9a08498e0b53e2]."

(source: Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory and Non-Compensatory Decision-Making Techniques.md)

## 10. Consider non-compensatory techniques to verify no option violates a hard constraint

Also apply a non-compensatory check (dominance or satisficing) to confirm that no candidate fails a minimum threshold on any critical criterion — for example, an unacceptable security posture or an unusable licence type — regardless of how well it scores elsewhere. Non-compensatory techniques "do not allow tradeoffs among the criteria."

(source: Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory and Non-Compensatory Decision-Making Techniques.md)

## 11. Use multiple estimation techniques for uncertain costs and risks, and look for convergence

For criteria that must be estimated (e.g., integration effort, expected defect rate), "[SWEBOK excerpt a4ee48bb5cbb175f227a]." Look for convergence among estimates; divergence signals that an important factor may have been overlooked.

## 12. Perform sensitivity analysis on the weights and scores

Because many scores and weights are themselves estimates, check whether the preferred choice changes when weights shift within plausible ranges. If the same candidate wins across a wide range of weight combinations, the choice is robust. If it is sensitive to small changes, that uncertainty should be disclosed to stakeholders.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 4b2f6ebfdcf3c83c2b23]")
