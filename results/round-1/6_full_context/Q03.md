1. **Include all information that led to the exception in the exception message.** This helps developers diagnose the cause of the problem during debugging and maintenance.

## Alternative Error-Handling Techniques

7. **Returning a neutral value** is one alternative when an error occurs — the routine returns a benign, do-nothing value rather than failing outright.
(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 25fb1a8cce8efbace92b].")

8. **Substituting the next piece of valid data** allows processing to continue when one data item is bad, by skipping the problematic input and moving on.
(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 25fb1a8cce8efbace92b].")

9. **Logging a warning message** records the error without necessarily stopping execution, enabling post-hoc analysis.
(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 25fb1a8cce8efbace92b].")

10. **Returning an error code** signals the calling routine that something went wrong, delegating the decision about how to respond to a higher level.
(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 25fb1a8cce8efbace92b].")

11. **Shutting down the software** is an appropriate response in high-integrity or safety-critical systems where continuing in a corrupt state is unacceptable.

12. **Back up and retry** as a fault tolerance strategy: when a fault is detected, restore to a previous good state and attempt the operation again.
