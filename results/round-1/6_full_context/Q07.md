# Evaluating Test Suite Fault Detection Beyond Code Coverage

Here is a list of guidelines drawn from the SWEBOK:

---

## 1. Use Mutation Testing to Measure Fault Detection Effectiveness

Create mutants (slightly modified versions of the software under test) and measure what fraction your test suite "kills." The mutation score — the ratio of killed mutants to total generated mutants — directly measures the test suite's ability to find faults. The higher the mutation score, the better the fault-detection ability.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt c55903435d9db2cf30b2]." — Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation)

---

## 2. Apply Fault Injection to Estimate Remaining Genuine Faults

Artificially introduce faults into the system under test before running your suite. By observing which injected faults are found and which are not, you can evaluate testing effectiveness and estimate the number of remaining genuine faults.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 19b4787cc787bf38cb63]." — Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation)

---

## 3. Use Fault Type Classifications and Defect Statistics to Focus Testing

Study past defect data and fault taxonomies (such as orthogonal defect classification) to understand which fault types are most common in your domain. Use this knowledge to judge whether your suite targets those high-frequency fault categories.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt d626790ac088b551641e]." — Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/SUT Measurements and Fault Classification for Test Planning)

---

## 4. Measure Fault Density and Use Reliability Growth Models

Track discovered faults as a ratio to software size (fault density) and apply reliability growth models to predict whether the software is mature enough for release. These models tell you whether continued testing is likely to surface more faults.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt fa99d1606566f5056a0c]." — Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Software Reliability Evaluation in Cloud Testing)

---

## 5. Compare the Relative Effectiveness of Different Techniques Applied to Your Suite

Evaluate your suite by comparing it against alternative test generation techniques using metrics [SWEBOK excerpt 8a0d9ea68d3e5e67ba92] faults found through testing to all faults found during and after testing, and reliability improvement.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt c4ad890b5668c6dfc1ec]." — Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Test Effectiveness and Process Levels/Test Suite Effectiveness and Testing Process Integration)

---

## 6. Assess Functional Coverage Against Requirements, Not Just Structural Coverage

Evaluate the percentage of functional requirements exercised by your test suite (not only code branches). A suite that covers code but misses requirements may miss entire classes of real faults.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt a3145eefba8df273f029]." — Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Test Measurement and Coverage Analysis/Coverage Measures in Software Testing Techniques)

---

## 7. Combine Multiple Complementary Test Techniques

No single technique reveals all faults. Evaluate whether your suite combines specification-based (black-box), structure-based (white-box), and experience-based techniques, since they use different information sources and find different classes of faults.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 5957a5d79fa90b756cf0]." — Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Testing Approaches and Test Case Selection Methods/Combining Functional and Structural Testing Techniques)

---

## 8. Conduct Thorough Regression Testing and Track Fault Escape Rate

Evaluate how many faults escape to later stages or to production (post-delivery failures). A good suite should prevent fault escape; tracking post-delivery costs and external failure rates reveals weaknesses in fault-detection effectiveness.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 052415f5554eefaa5c51]." — Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Formal Methods, Agile Development, and Software Quality/Nonconformance Costs_ Pre and Post-Delivery/Post-Delivery Software Failure Costs)

---

## 9. Use Defect Detection Percentage and Process Measures to Monitor Effectiveness

Track the defect detection percentage across test phases and combine it with other process metrics such as residual risk and cumulative defects open/closed, to gain a holistic view of how effective the suite is at finding faults early.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 66ee5f294fbd9a42b438]." — Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Test Management and Process Monitoring/Test Team Organization and Process Measures)

---

## 10. Evaluate Test Reusability and Knowledge Capture for Long-Term Fault Detection

Assess whether your test cases are well-organized and reusable so that as requirements change, the suite continues to detect regressions and newly introduced faults effectively.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 5f0cbdf771994169746a]." — Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Test Completion and Knowledge Management/Test Completion and Artifact Reusability in Software Testing)
