### Question

What kinds of changes are made to software that is already in use, and what distinguishes each kind from the others?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

Here are the kinds of changes made to software already in use, with what distinguishes each:

1. **Corrective maintenance** addresses faults and latent defects in operational software. It is reactive — triggered by observed failures or defects in the software as it runs in production. (source: Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Maintenance Roles and Cost Distribution/Software Maintenance Activities and Cost Distribution.md)

2. **Preventive maintenance** anticipates future problems, such as security threats or technical obsolescence, before they cause failures. It differs from corrective maintenance in that it acts proactively rather than in response to an observed defect. (source: Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Maintenance Roles and Cost Distribution/Software Maintenance Activities and Cost Distribution.md)

3. **Adaptive maintenance** modifies software to accommodate changes in interfaced systems or infrastructure — that is, the software itself may not be broken, but the environment around it has changed and the software must be updated to remain compatible. (source: Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Maintenance Roles and Cost Distribution/Software Maintenance Activities and Cost Distribution.md)

4. **Additive maintenance** [SWEBOK excerpt 8aba2087ad1e502a2bbd] [SWEBOK excerpt 70576c4a1bec216f1bb0] after delivery. It is distinguished from perfective maintenance by the scale and nature of the additions — it involves notably larger additions or changes. (source: Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Types of Software Maintenance Activities.md)

5. **Perfective maintenance** modifies the software after delivery to provide enhancements for users, improve program documentation, or recode [SWEBOK excerpt 3a7ea8de5455f72dc7ec]. It differs from additive maintenance in that it involves improvements or enhancements of a more modest scope rather than relatively large additions. (source: Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Types of Software Maintenance Activities.md)

6. **Emergency maintenance** is [SWEBOK excerpt 29afddbf422c4d134083]. It is distinguished from corrective maintenance by being temporary and unplanned — a stopgap measure rather than a full fix. (source: Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Types of Software Maintenance Activities.md)

7. Studies and surveys indicate [SWEBOK excerpt e71240f6dc822f205983]**, not merely for fixing faults. This means the majority of maintenance effort falls into the perfective, additive, and adaptive categories rather than the corrective category, which contradicts common perception. (source: Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Maintenance Roles and Cost Distribution/Software Maintenance Activities and Cost Distribution.md)
