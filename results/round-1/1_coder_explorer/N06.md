### Question

At the end of each release our bug tracker tells us only how many bugs were found, and that count gives the team nothing to act on. What should we do with our bug records so that they help us introduce fewer bugs in later releases?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
A raw defect count gives no actionable information. SWEBOK v4 says you should classify every bug with a taxonomy, compute richer metrics (fault density, fault depth, fault multiplicity), apply statistical and trend analysis to find the most frequent defect types, perform root cause analysis to eliminate causes rather than just symptoms, store the knowledge in reusable repositories, and feed all of this back into test planning and process improvement for the next release.

# Guidelines

- **Establish a defect classification taxonomy.** Do not store bugs as undifferentiated counts; "[SWEBOK excerpt bcc82cdbad3e6dcf9632]." Without categorization, "data on errors and defects … may be lost unless they are recorded."
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/Software Quality Management and Defect Classification.md`)

- **Perform Root Cause Analysis (RCA).** After classifying, analyze and summarize findings to find root causes, then use measurement techniques to improve engineering processes, techniques, and tools — not merely remove the individual defect.
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Quality Management and Defect Classification.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 866261af5d5a2dcf15a3].")

- **Record fault types and their relative frequency.** "[SWEBOK excerpt 0202c8cf18285fa722ab]."
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/SUT Measurements and Fault Classification for Test Planning.md`, §4.1.2)

- **Compute fault density and richer defect metrics, not just raw counts.** Calculate faults found per unit of SUT size, plus fault depth (minimum removals to make the system correct) and fault multiplicity ([SWEBOK excerpt b9f3477d4f96f3fae60b]).
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/…/Fault Density Measurement in Software Testing.md`, §4.1.3)

- **Apply statistical and trend analysis to defect records.** "Results from trend analysis may indicate [SWEBOK excerpt 9656e6fe86cfea371278]." Also, "software quality measurement … [SWEBOK excerpt ddd77427515d2f2ed0b5]."
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Quality Measurement and Statistical Analysis Techniques.md`)

- **Visualize defect data to focus improvement efforts.** "[SWEBOK excerpt b082b69560e2d01b872b]."
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Quality Measurement and Statistical Analysis Techniques.md`)

- **Set measurable quality thresholds against which defect data is judged.** "[SWEBOK excerpt 6cb44ce49b52695c8694]."
  (source: `root/Software Quality Management and Project Oversight/Software Quality Planning and Management Challenges.md`)

- **Feed defect measures back into test planning (shift-left).** Measures such as change failure rate and MTTR derived from defect records "are [SWEBOK excerpt 97562cd45f0cc53ce935]."
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/…/SUT Measurements and Fault Classification for Test Planning.md`, §4.1.1)

- **Store defect and test-result knowledge in searchable repositories for reuse.** "[SWEBOK excerpt 5f0cbdf771994169746a]."
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/…/Test Completion and Artifact Reusability in Software Testing.md`, §5.1.9)

- **Communicate defect reports to all relevant stakeholders.** "Test results are communicated to relevant stakeholders" at stage completion — ensure the people who can act on the data actually receive it.
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/…/Test Completion and Documentation in Test Management.md`, §5.1.8)
