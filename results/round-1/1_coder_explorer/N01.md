### Question

A feature request I have to implement is written as loose prose, and the product owner and I keep reading it differently. How should the team pin down, together with the domain experts and before any code is written, what the feature must do, so that it cannot be misread?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
Before any code is written, the team and domain experts should collaboratively move from loose prose to precise, unambiguous specifications through a combination of facilitated elicitation sessions (workshops, interviews, user story mapping), structured writing disciplines (actor-action templates, behavioral models), and — most powerfully — by jointly agreeing on concrete acceptance test cases that must pass before coding can begin (ATDD). Every resulting requirement should be unambiguous, testable/quantified, atomic, and use stakeholder vocabulary, and the whole set must be validated through multi-perspective reviews and optionally prototyping.

# Guidelines

- **Involve all stakeholder perspectives in specification and review.** Clients/users check that their needs are accurately represented; domain-expert engineers check clarity; construction engineers check sufficiency.
(source: `root/Requirements Validation and Management/Requirements Validation Methods and Techniques.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 512c490da0c5917a0c27]")

- **Run facilitated elicitation sessions (JAD/JRP workshops, brainstorming meetings) rather than relying on a single prose document.**
(source: `root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Stakeholder Identification and Requirements Elicitation Techniques/Stakeholder Elicitation Techniques and Requirements Sources.md` — "joint application development (JAD), joint requirements planning (JRP) and other facilitated workshops")

- **Apply the 5-Whys technique to surface the true underlying need.** Repeatedly ask "Why is this the requirement?" until [SWEBOK excerpt d7d0175f8ce126234e03]." This prevents implementing a suboptimal solution to a misunderstood problem.
(source: `root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md` — "The 5-whys technique [SWEBOK excerpt 20f776590023e6560f73]")

- **Write each requirement so it is unambiguous (interpretable in only one way), testable/quantified, atomic, expressed in stakeholder vocabulary, and acceptable to all stakeholders.**
(source: `root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 3f9aa2c4f8f16bd73d76]")

- **Replace or supplement free-form prose with structured natural language** using actor-action format (who does what), to increase precision and conciseness.
(source: `root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Natural Language Requirements Specifications/Structured Natural Language Requirements Specification.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 4eecb83f896611f6153d]")

- **Model the feature's behaviour explicitly** using use cases, state models, UML activity diagrams, or interaction diagrams to make implicit assumptions visible and reduce ambiguity.
(source: `root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Behavioral Models and Requirements Documentation/Behavioral Modeling Approaches and Formality Levels/Behavioral Models and Formality Levels in Requirements Specification.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 962a07283f1ee78a1996] include use case modeling, interaction diagrams, state modeling, UML activity diagrams and data-flow modeling")

- **Agree on concrete acceptance test cases before any production code is written (ATDD/BDD).** [SWEBOK excerpt 616ab5625ac2e0b3053a] QA professionals meet [SWEBOK excerpt 89edc5b9a7727dcde029] feature has been correctly implemented — and no code is written until this agreement exists.
(source: `root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Test-Driven Development and Acceptance Testing/Acceptance Criteria-Based Requirements Specification.md` — "meet before any production design or [SWEBOK excerpt acf2737e4c17b2e47a7b]")

- **Build a prototype** to demonstrate the engineer's interpretation of the requirements, expose hidden assumptions, and get concrete feedback from stakeholders (especially for dynamic UI behaviour that cannot easily be captured in text).
(source: `root/Requirements Validation and Management/Prototyping for Requirements Validation and Feedback.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 78d5d553044949c2fea4]")

- **Conduct a multi-reviewer inspection** of the resulting requirements document, using checklists or a "definition of done" to focus reviewers on errors, omissions, invalid assumptions, and lack of clarity.
(source: `root/Requirements Validation and Management/Requirements Validation Methods and Techniques.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 76eb5a3a9492756ef3e6] practice")

- **Annotate each requirement with its rationale, source stakeholder, and acceptance criteria** so future readers understand not just *what* is required but *why*, reducing the chance of reinterpretation.
(source: `root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Behavioral Models and Requirements Documentation/Formal Requirements Models and Additional Attributes.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 59d7cf197b35b182a65d]); … acceptance criteria")
