### Question

How can a routine make its assumptions about inputs and outputs explicit and guard itself against callers passing it values it was not built to accept?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
SWEBOK v4 explains that a routine makes its assumptions explicit and guards itself against invalid inputs through three interrelated techniques: **assertions** (executable predicates that check conditions at runtime), **design by contract** (preconditions and postconditions that formally define what a routine expects and guarantees), and **defensive programming** (explicitly checking every input parameter's value and deciding what to do when inputs are bad). These are listed among the primary quality techniques of software construction, alongside structured exception handling.

# Guidelines

- **Use assertions to state assumptions at runtime.** Assertions are executable predicates placed in a routine; they are especially useful in high-reliability programs and help [SWEBOK excerpt 9289e01cb9565232f210]." They are compiled in during development and compiled out for production. (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Assertions and Design by Contract in Programming.md`)

- **Adopt design by contract — write explicit preconditions and postconditions for each routine.** "[SWEBOK excerpt f5cbc0b15f96051e15ac]." (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Design by Contract and Defensive Programming.md`)

- **Apply defensive programming — check the values of all input parameters.** "[SWEBOK excerpt 798f1f64b64a6bf2581f]." (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Design by Contract and Defensive Programming.md`)

- **Choose an explicit strategy for handling bad inputs.** Options include [SWEBOK excerpt 86e16ba9355168288551], returning an error code, or shutting down. "[SWEBOK excerpt 5cbc324f523e1430b28c]." (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

- **Use exceptions with a deliberately designed policy.** When a routine detects an invalid condition it cannot handle, it should throw an exception. Exception-handling policies should follow [SWEBOK excerpt a3a14af0d9c4046cd376]." (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

- **Recognise assertions and defensive programming as first-class quality techniques.** The BoK lists "use of assertions and defensive programming" explicitly among the primary techniques for ensuring construction quality, alongside unit testing, test-first development, inspections, and static analysis. (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Construction Quality Techniques and Practices.md`)

- **Construct the routine so its behaviour can be verified independently.** "[SWEBOK excerpt f84716b6c660c784fa16]." Techniques include following coding standards and restricting complex language structures. (source: `root/Construction Practices, Standards, and Development Models/Constructing for Verification and Reusing Assets.md`)

# Gaps
- The BoK does not detail **class-level invariants** separately from routine-level contracts beyond naming them as part of the contract concept.
- No guidance on specific assertion frameworks or language-level tools.
- **Static typing** as a compile-time mechanism for enforcing input assumptions is not discussed.
