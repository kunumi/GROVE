What kinds of changes are made to software that is already in use, and what distinguishes each kind from the others?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
SWEBOK v4 identifies five distinct kinds of changes made to software already in use: **corrective**, **adaptive**, **perfective**, **additive**, and **emergency** maintenance. Each is distinguished by *why* the change is made—to fix a fault, to respond to an environmental change, to improve existing features, to add substantially new features, or to temporarily keep a system running while a proper fix is prepared.

# Guidelines

- **Corrective maintenance** targets actual faults that cause the software to operate incorrectly; it is distinguished from all other types because a defect exists that must be repaired. (source: `root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Types of Software Maintenance Activities.md` — listed as a distinct category alongside perfective, adaptive, additive, and emergency maintenance)

- **Adaptive maintenance** keeps software usable when its surrounding environment changes (e.g., new hardware or OS); the software is not broken, but external conditions have shifted. (source: `root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Types of Software Maintenance Activities.md` — adaptive maintenance listed as a distinct category)

- **Perfective maintenance** is *"[SWEBOK excerpt b3f0980f7367b3ca8843]."* It improves what already exists without adding wholly new capabilities. (source: `root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Types of Software Maintenance Activities.md`)

- **Additive maintenance** differs from perfective in two ways: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 5bd62031f86d85aeedfe]."* In short, it involves large-scale new functionality rather than small improvements. (source: `root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Types of Software Maintenance Activities.md`)

- **Emergency maintenance** is an *"[SWEBOK excerpt 972c8f1f2f359acc9f95]."* It is distinguished from corrective maintenance by being a temporary stopgap, not a permanent fix.
