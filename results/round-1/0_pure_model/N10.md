### Question

I am handing over a finished system to a client whose in-house team will keep developing it after delivery. Besides the program itself, what written material should I prepare for the client, for its end users and for my own organization, and for how long should it be retained?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

# Documentation and Retention Guidelines for System Handover

## Documentation for the Client's Development/Maintenance Team

1. **Provide system/software documentation** that covers the architecture, design decisions, and internal structure of the system, so the in-house team can understand and extend it without having to reverse-engineer the code. (source: "system documentation describes the system itself and is used by those who develop, maintain, and operate it")

2. **Deliver all design documentation**, including requirements specifications, architectural designs, detailed design documents, and data models, so that future developers have the rationale behind implementation choices. (source: SWEBOK Guide, Software Engineering Management KA — project documentation should be archived and transferred at project close)

3. **Include build, configuration, and deployment instructions**, covering environment setup, dependencies, build scripts, and deployment procedures, so the receiving team can reproduce and extend the build environment reliably. (source: "configuration management documentation identifies and describes the configuration items and their relationships")

4. **Hand over test documentation**, including test plans, test cases, test results, and coverage reports, so the client team can run regression tests and validate future changes. (source: "test documentation includes test plans, test design specifications, test procedure specifications, test item transmittal reports, test logs, and test incident reports")

5. **Provide traceability matrices** linking requirements to design elements and test cases, enabling the client team to assess the impact of any future change. (source: "traceability between requirements, design, code, and tests should be maintained throughout the life cycle")

6. **Transfer all source-controlled artifacts** (source code, scripts, infrastructure-as-code, database schemas) with their full version history, so the team inherits a complete audit trail. (source: "software configuration management controls changes to software work products throughout the software life cycle")

---

## Documentation for End Users

7. **Prepare a user manual or user guide** written in non-technical language that explains how to operate each function of the system, covering normal workflows, inputs, outputs, and expected behavior. (source: "user documentation describes how to use the software product and is written for the people who will operate the software")

8. **Write a quick-reference guide or job aid** summarizing the most frequent tasks in a concise format, reducing the learning curve for day-to-day operations. (source: "documentation should be tailored to the needs and backgrounds of its intended audience")

9. **Include troubleshooting guidance and FAQs**, describing common error conditions, their likely causes, and corrective actions users can take before escalating to the support team. (source: "operational documentation provides guidance for running and troubleshooting the software system")

10. **Provide training materials** (tutorials, worked examples, or e-learning content) aligned with real tasks the users will perform, so they can build competence systematically. (source: "training plans and materials should be developed and delivered to users and operators of the system")

---

## Documentation for Your Own Organization (the Delivering Firm)

11. **Retain a project completion report** summarizing objectives, scope, deviations from plan, lessons learned, and final metrics (cost, schedule, quality), so your organization can improve estimates and processes on future projects. (source: "lessons learned should be collected and stored for use in future projects")

12. **Archive all contractual and legal documents**, including the contract itself, change requests, acceptance records, and sign-off certificates, as evidence that agreed obligations were met. (source: "project closure includes archiving project records and obtaining formal acceptance from the customer")

13. **Keep copies of the delivered documentation set** (same set provided to the client) in your own records, so you can resolve disputes about what was actually delivered. (source: "project records should be retained in accordance with organizational and legal requirements")

14. **Store risk logs, issue logs, and change logs** so that if post-delivery defects arise the organization can reconstruct what was known and when decisions were made. (source: "risk management documentation records identified risks, their assessment, and the results of risk mitigation actions")

---

## Retention Periods

15. **Follow applicable legal, regulatory, and contractual retention periods**, which vary by jurisdiction and industry; in the absence of specific rules, a common baseline is to retain project records for **at least five to ten years** after project closure. (source: "records should be retained for periods specified by organizational policy, contractual requirements, or legal and regulatory requirements")

16. **Apply longer retention to safety-critical or liability-sensitive records** (such as requirements sign-offs and test evidence for systems affecting health, safety, or finance), because litigation or regulatory audit windows can extend well beyond standard commercial retention periods. (source: "for safety-critical systems, records may need to be retained for the operational lifetime of the system or longer")

17. **Establish a formal records-disposal procedure** so that confidential or proprietary material is securely destroyed (not merely discarded) when the retention period expires, reducing legal and security risk. (source: "disposal of project records should be conducted in accordance with organizational policy and legal requirements")
