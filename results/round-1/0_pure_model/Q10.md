### Question

How can a routine make its assumptions about inputs and outputs explicit and guard itself against callers passing it values it was not built to accept?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

Here are guidelines a routine can follow to make its assumptions explicit and guard against invalid inputs/outputs:

1. **Use preconditions to state what must be true before the routine executes.** Preconditions document the caller's obligations—the values, ranges, or states the routine requires to operate correctly. (source: "preconditions: properties that must hold when a routine is called")

2. **Use postconditions to state what the routine guarantees upon completion.** Postconditions document what the routine promises to deliver, making output assumptions explicit to both callers and maintainers. (source: "postconditions: properties that the routine guarantees on return")

3. **Apply assertions (or assertion mechanisms) to check preconditions and postconditions at runtime.** Assertions are executable checks that verify assumptions during development and testing, catching violations early. (source: "assertions: executable predicates that are placed in a program to allow runtime checks of the program")

4. **Design by Contract (DbC) formally binds preconditions, postconditions, and invariants into the routine's interface.** This technique makes the contract between caller and callee explicit and enforceable. (source: "design by contract: an approach to building software based on the metaphor of a business contract between a client and a supplier")

5. **Define and enforce class or module invariants** so that the object's internal state remains consistent before and after every public routine call. (source: "invariants: properties that must hold for all instances of a class or module")

6. **Validate inputs defensively** (defensive programming) when the routine cannot rely on callers to honor its preconditions, checking parameters and failing safely rather than producing incorrect results. (source: "defensive programming: the practice of anticipating that errors will occur and coding accordingly")

7. **Document assumptions in the routine's interface specification**, so callers understand required input constraints without having to read the implementation. (source: "the routine's interface should be clearly documented including assumptions about inputs and outputs")
