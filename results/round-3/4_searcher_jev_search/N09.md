## Guidelines for Choosing Between Three Candidate Libraries Using Multiple Criteria

---

### 1. Frame the decision as a multiple-attribute problem from the outset

Acknowledge explicitly that money (license cost) is usually the most important financial criterion but is rarely the only one. Criteria that cannot be expressed in monetary terms—maturity, community activity, team familiarity—are legitimate decision attributes and must be incorporated, not ignored.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt a29c4eeacc2005294c93] … [SWEBOK excerpt b4a03fe3cebc8941c34c]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Multiple-Attribute Decision-Making Beyond Financial Criteria.md)

---

### 2. Define and limit your selection criteria deliberately

Name each criterion explicitly before evaluating any library. Keep the list disciplined: too many criteria make the analysis unwieldy; too few may fail to differentiate the candidates and can lead to a suboptimal choice. Where possible, express each criterion objectively—even non-monetary ones.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt ad779e7ae7316b9713b2]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multiple-Attribute Decision Criteria and Evaluation.md)

---

### 3. Balance the number of criteria against the effort required to analyze them

Adding more criteria improves decision quality, but each criterion adds analysis cost. The benefit of including an additional criterion must justify the extra effort needed to evaluate it rigorously across all candidates.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 6e92682341c43c595e4b]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Establishing Criteria and Evaluation Methods/Balancing Decision Criteria Against Analysis Effort.md)

---

### 4. Apply a consistent basis of comparison to all three candidates

Evaluate every library against every criterion from the same viewpoint, over the same planning horizon, and considering the same categories of cost and benefit. Applying a longer time horizon to one candidate than another will artificially favor the one evaluated over the shorter period.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 52782f508d83e0a6b786] … [SWEBOK excerpt 8c6be2eafa754bc08485]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Establishing Criteria and Evaluation Methods/Evaluating Alternatives Against Selection Criteria.md)

---

### 5. Treat intangible factors (team familiarity, community, maturity) as formal decision inputs

Team familiarity, community activity, and library maturity are intangible assets—non-monetary knowledge assets that nonetheless affect your project's success and your organization's capability. They must be identified, assigned a qualitative importance weight (e.g., 1–5), and carried into the decision alongside financial data.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 1b7568245a34d0519644] … [SWEBOK excerpt 5ef9c6bfb6cb51f869e8]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Evaluating Complex Proposals and Strategic Alternatives/Do-Nothing Alternative and Intangible Assets in Decision-Making.md)

---

### 6. Use a compensatory technique to collapse all criteria into a defensible single figure of merit

When trade-offs across criteria are acceptable (e.g., a weaker community score could be offset by much lower cost), use a **compensatory** multi-attribute technique. Practical options include **nondimensional scaling**, **additive weighting**, or the **Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)**—all of which reduce all criteria to a single comparable figure.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt dd90612de75ae5a7ee4c])." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory and Non-Compensatory Decision-Making Techniques.md)

---

### 7. Use a non-compensatory technique when certain thresholds must not be violated

If any criterion is a hard constraint—e.g., the library must have an OSI-approved license, or performance must exceed a minimum threshold—use a **non-compensatory** technique such as **satisficing** (eliminate any candidate that fails the threshold) or **lexicography** (rank criteria in priority order and prefer the candidate that wins on the most important one). These do not allow a high score on one criterion to rescue a failure on another.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt ceccdfe5823e12c32f0a]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory and Non-Compensatory Decision-Making Techniques.md)

---

### 8. Acknowledge and handle estimation uncertainty with ranges and sensitivity analysis

Scores on performance, maturity, and future community activity are estimates. When the degree of inaccuracy is high enough it could change the decision. Use ranges of estimates, perform sensitivity analysis on the weights and scores, and delay a final commitment if the data needed to resolve key uncertainties can be gathered cheaply.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt ec2285911628d5cd3988] … [SWEBOK excerpt cc452cd27b746130520e] [SWEBOK excerpt 4e41c08a45517283af71]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Estimation, Decision Analysis, and Investment Evaluation/Engineering Decision-Making Under Uncertainty and Risk.md)

---

### 9. Explicitly consider the "do-nothing" alternative

Before committing to any of the three libraries, ask whether the right answer is to use none of them—perhaps building a minimal in-house solution, waiting for a better option, or continuing with the current approach. The do-nothing alternative is not "[SWEBOK excerpt 0ba37f99d4b64f3ecd0e] outside this candidate set" and should be on the table in most decisions.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 0001d4911ad947890846]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Evaluating Complex Proposals and Strategic Alternatives/Do-Nothing Alternative and Intangible Assets in Decision-Making.md)

---

### 10. Assess supply-chain, license, and security risks as explicit criteria

When evaluating third-party libraries, treat license obligations, vulnerability exposure, and dependency chain risks as formal selection criteria alongside cost and performance. License conflicts and unfiltered access to external repositories are known risk vectors that should be evaluated with appropriate technical or procedural controls.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt e3ca00f82a82d0e84520] … [SWEBOK excerpt 4e4767041d65392d59ca]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Quality Management and Project Oversight/Software Acquisition and Project Oversight/Software Acquisition and Supplier Contract Management.md and /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Methods and Languages/Dependency Management and Construction Practices/Managing Software Dependencies and Supply Chain Risks.md)

---

### 11. Document the rationale, including why the other candidates were rejected

Record the criteria used, the weights assigned, the scores for each library, the trade-offs considered, and—critically—why the two rejected libraries were not chosen. This record prevents the team from revisiting a rejected option for forgotten reasons; it also allows the decision to be reopened legitimately if relevant conditions (e.g., licensing terms, community activity) change later.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt a7e4160af8574fe372e0]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Foundations of Software Architecture/Software Architecture_ Foundations, Views, and Patterns/Architectural Design Languages, Decisions, and Technical Debt/Documenting Architectural Decisions and Their Rationale.md)

---

### 12. Monitor the chosen library's actual performance against the estimates used in the decision

After selecting a library, track whether its actual performance, maintenance burden, and community support match the estimates you relied on. Comparing estimates to actual outcomes closes the loop, improves future estimates, and may trigger a reconsideration if the selected library consistently underperforms the forecast.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 6380fd8dc649e0c7b56a]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Estimation, Decision Analysis, and Investment Evaluation/Monitor Performance of Selected Alternative.md)
