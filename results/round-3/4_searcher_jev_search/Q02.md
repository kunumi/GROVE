## Guidelines for Replacing a Long Conditional Chain with a Table-Driven Approach

The SWEBOK identifies **table-driven construction** as the recommended approach when a function has grown into a long chain of conditionals that maps combinations of conditions to results.

---

### 1. Recognise when table-driven code is appropriate
Apply a table-driven approach when the conditional logic has grown complex enough that a table would be simpler and safer to maintain than the equivalent `if/else` or `case` structure.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 1f02a9a5cf54055acd0d]."*
> (source: /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Runtime Configuration and Internationalization in Software Design.md)

---

### 2. Decide what information to store in the table
Before building the table, explicitly decide which data — conditions, results, actions, or mappings — belongs in it. This shapes the entire structure of the solution.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 94a3ffa3aaf39c2372b6]…"*
> (source: /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Runtime Configuration and Internationalization in Software Design.md)

### 3. Decide how to efficiently access the table
The second key decision is the access strategy: how the code will look up the right entry given a set of inputs. The access method (whether entries are reached directly by an index, through an intermediate mapping, or by scanning ranges) must be chosen deliberately for correctness and performance.
