## Guidelines for Making a Routine's Assumptions Explicit and Guarding Against Invalid Inputs

---

### 1. Establish a contract for every routine using preconditions and postconditions

Specify **preconditions** (what must be true of inputs before the routine runs) and **postconditions** (what the routine guarantees about its outputs when it finishes). This turns the routine's assumptions into an explicit, enforceable agreement with its callers.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 3371d5be97fc67ae3e08]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Assertions and Design by Contract in Programming.md`)

---

### 2. Use contracts to precisely specify and clarify a routine's semantics

Write the contract so that it completely describes what the routine does — not just the happy path, but the boundaries of acceptable behavior. A precise contract reduces ambiguity for callers and maintainers alike.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 24a838ff86d1790c3ee8]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Design by Contract and Defensive Programming.md`)

---

### 3. Apply defensive programming to protect a routine from invalid inputs

Do not assume callers will always pass correct values. Actively protect the routine by treating every input as potentially bad until verified — this is the core principle of defensive programming.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt fe09ccfb26565a8b97e8]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Design by Contract and Defensive Programming.md`)

---

### 4. Check the values of all input parameters and decide how to handle bad inputs

As the first line of defence inside the routine, inspect every incoming parameter. Decide in advance — per the routine's contract — what constitutes an invalid value and what action should follow.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 93d62d9d3fbab9385d19]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Design by Contract and Defensive Programming.md`)

---

### 5. Use assertions to enforce assumptions and detect violations at runtime

Place executable assertions at the entry and exit points of a routine (and at critical internal points) to verify that assumptions hold. Assertions turn implicit assumptions into visible, automatically checked properties.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 5911453b0a4f5e43261b]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Parameterization, Templates, Generics, and Assertions.md`)

---

### 6. Use assertions especially in high-reliability code, and compile them out for production

Rely on assertions most heavily where correctness is critical. Activate them during development to catch violations early; they can then be compiled out to avoid degrading runtime performance in production builds.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 4b5d09b3a87e6f492d8f]. […] [SWEBOK excerpt fc8cffb24cc1696f5f4f]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Assertions and Design by Contract in Programming.md`)

---

### 7. Leverage assertions to surface mismatched interface assumptions quickly

Assertions do more than check values — they expose disagreements between what a caller believes it is passing and what the routine actually expects, catching integration errors that would otherwise be hard to trace.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 2ba288231ac4fcb1b65f]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Assertions and Design by Contract in Programming.md`)

---

### 8. When an invalid input is detected, choose an appropriate error-handling response

The routine should not silently continue. Depending on context, valid responses include: [SWEBOK excerpt 86e16ba9355168288551], returning an error code, or shutting down.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 25fb1a8cce8efbace92b]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

---

### 9. Use exceptions to signal and propagate detected errors to callers

When a violation of the routine's contract is detected, throw an exception rather than returning a silent bad value. The caller can then catch and handle the erroneous condition or escalate it further.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt ea790ec47590a1c6d2e8] try-catch [SWEBOK excerpt 980c1713bcc05a3281c2]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

---

### 10. Follow principled exception-handling policies consistently across the codebase

Design exception handling carefully and uniformly: include all diagnostic context in the exception message, avoid empty catch blocks, know what exceptions library calls may throw, consider a centralized exception reporter, and standardize usage.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 8a98e7d1cb740854b979]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

---

### 11. Address both anticipated and exceptional error conditions in code

A routine's coding must account for bad input explicitly — not just the normal path. Preventing [SWEBOK excerpt bf1272a0069f7fe66f63], array index violations) caused by unchecked inputs is an integral part of this.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 59d5fa3ccf0dc673311d])." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Software Coding and Construction Testing Practices.md`)

---

### 12. Treat the use of assertions and defensive programming as primary construction-quality techniques

Do not view these mechanisms as optional extras. The SWEBOK lists them alongside unit testing, code reviews, and static analysis as primary techniques for ensuring quality during construction.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt e46a3561a4f33e3d466c]: […] Use of assertions and defensive programming […]" — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Construction Quality Techniques and Practices.md`)
