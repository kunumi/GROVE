## Kinds of Changes Made to Software Already in Use

The SWEBOK identifies six distinct types of software maintenance—each distinguished by its trigger and goal.

---

### 1. Corrective Maintenance
**Guideline:** Apply corrective maintenance when a fault or failure is discovered in operational software; the goal is to remove the defect that caused the problem.

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Types of Software Maintenance Activities.md`)

---

### 2. Adaptive Maintenance
**Guideline:** Apply adaptive maintenance when the environment surrounding the software changes (e.g., a new operating system, database, or interfaced system); the modification keeps the software usable in its new context rather than fixing a fault or adding a feature.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt ebc3b37ef414b02f8fb9]" — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/…/Software Maintenance Activities and Cost Distribution.md`)

---

### 3. Perfective Maintenance
**Guideline:** Apply perfective maintenance to enhance software that already works correctly—by adding user-requested improvements, recoding for better performance, or improving documentation—without necessarily adding large new functional blocks.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt e5fe30859351837b8f04].")

---

### 4. Additive Maintenance
**Guideline:** Distinguish additive maintenance from perfective maintenance when the change delivers **new** functions or features; additive maintenance involves relatively large additions or changes that improve usability, performance, or maintainability, whereas perfective maintenance involves smaller refinements to existing capabilities.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt f5ac630d0543a2ba3a3b].")

---

### 5. Preventive Maintenance
**Guideline:** Apply preventive maintenance proactively to detect and correct latent defects before they surface as operational failures, thereby reducing the likelihood of future faults—not in response to any current failure.

(source: "Prevent security threats" and "Correct faults and latent defects" — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/…/Software Maintenance Activities and Cost Distribution.md`)

---

### 6. Emergency Maintenance
**Guideline:** Use emergency maintenance only as a temporary, unscheduled measure to keep a system operational when a critical failure occurs; it is explicitly pending a proper corrective maintenance action and is not a permanent fix.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 3dca8108d3ba2c28d925].")

---

### Key Distinguishing Principle
**Guideline:** Recognize that corrective maintenance accounts for a minority of total maintenance effort—studies show **over 80% of maintenance is used for enhancing and adapting software**, not fixing bugs—and that conflating categories in management reports leads to cost misconceptions.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 0fcd2b20536e771d4fea]" — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/…/Software Maintenance Activities and Cost Distribution.md`)
