### PART A — What the write-up must state

---

**1. State the required inputs, the transforming activities, and the outputs produced.**

The write-up must cover the three structural pillars of any process description: what enters, what happens to it, and what comes out. Because your workflow passes work from person to person, every hand-off is itself an output that becomes the next step's input, so each transition must be described.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 47bd2c965711c92d2c7a] … [SWEBOK excerpt 0abe0273b7db701637cc]."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Engineering Processes and System Lifecycles/Software Engineering Process Definition and Structure/Software Process Definition and Components.md)

---

**2. State the controls (directives and constraints) and the enabling mechanisms (tools, technologies, resources, infrastructure) that govern each step.**

It is not enough to list activities; the write-up must also capture what constrains or empowers execution at each step, so that any performer — human or automated — knows the boundary conditions.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 8a02b8f495be0b31bbdf]."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Engineering Processes and System Lifecycles/Software Engineering Process Definition and Structure/Software Process Definition and Components.md)

---

**3. Specify roles and responsibilities clearly, so that each participant knows what is expected of them during each activity.**

A workflow that only two senior developers carry in their heads is a knowledge risk. The write-up must name who does what and what authority or accountability they hold at each stage.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 33382b50a77a7f4ce944]."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/ISO 9001 and Software Quality Assurance Management Systems.md)

---

**4. State the associated constraints on each process, in addition to the activities themselves.**

Constraints define what must not be violated (timing dependencies, ordering rules, preconditions), which is precisely what distinguishes a reliable repeatable workflow from a loose informal custom.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 6444b726aad5aae95c31]."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Processes and Life Cycle Management/Software Process Definition and Life Cycle Specification.md)

---

**5. Make the specification easy to understand and correct, since it will serve as the basis for coordination, measurement, assessment, and quality management.**

Clarity is not a cosmetic virtue — it is a functional requirement. If new developers or automated agents misread the write-up, every execution of the workflow diverges from the intended process.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt e647bfb0d04baffa77f0]."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Processes and Life Cycle Management/Software Process Definition and Life Cycle Specification.md)

---

**6. Make the specification useful to humans so they can communicate with one another using it.**

A process specification that is too abstract or too technical for the people who will use it fails its primary purpose. The document must be legible to the full audience — including newcomers.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 3d38e79e462547e07bb3]."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Processes and Life Cycle Management/Software Process Definition and Life Cycle Specification.md)

---

**7. Document the activities and tasks [SWEBOK excerpt 06446d46b00905bafbe1] each key activity.**

"Steps" alone are insufficient. Each activity must be paired with its expected outcome, so that any executor — human or agent — can verify correct completion before passing work forward.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 399cd52c5115fe5a77d8]."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Quality Management Systems and Process Evaluation/Software Quality Management System Implementation and Evaluation.md)

---

**8. Write procedures that [SWEBOK excerpt 41713413346da5893a9e] each specific activity.**

A high-level description of the workflow is not sufficient for repeatability. Each activity needs a procedure — a step-by-step prescription — so that the same activity is performed identically regardless of who (or what) performs it.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 9c0494caf24bd8190773]."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Quality Management Systems and Process Evaluation/Software Quality Management System Implementation and Evaluation.md)

---

**9. Document the processes with the [SWEBOK excerpt f3b0fb2862a4c219e85d] to be verified.**

The write-up is not an internal artefact for its authors — it is a working tool for future practitioners. Writing with the user in mind and marking verification checkpoints makes the document operationally effective.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 9310ce2116f59aec8e4c] to be verified."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Quality Management Systems and Process Evaluation/Software Quality Management System Implementation and Evaluation.md)

---

**10. Capture preconditions (what must be true before a step executes) and postconditions (what is guaranteed to be true after it completes successfully).**

Preconditions prevent a step from starting in a bad state; postconditions confirm that the hand-off product is valid before the next person or agent picks it up. Both are essential for a workflow that passes work sequentially.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 135a69b5e6bb50e291a0] … [SWEBOK excerpt fb9c517ba81870bbd58a]."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Semantic Assumptions and Modeling Techniques/Preconditions, Postconditions, and Invariants in Functions.md)

### PART B — In what forms can the write-up be written?

---

**11. The write-up may be in natural language (plain prose), including structured natural language that imposes constraints on expression to increase precision and conciseness.**

Plain prose is accessible but can be ambiguous. Structured natural language (e.g., actor–action format: "When X occurs, \<role\> shall \<action\>") tightens the wording without requiring a formal notation.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt b31623575ab6bb052d27]."*

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 4eecb83f896611f6153d]."*

---

**12. The write-up may use graphical process-flow notations: data-flow diagrams, state charts, IDEF0, Petri nets, UML activity diagrams, or BPMN.**

Each of these notations represents the dynamics of the workflow visually, which makes sequence, branching, and concurrency easier to see at a glance than prose descriptions.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 3162f7a440e4e5df27e1] … [SWEBOK excerpt aa475b7014978ca83ca8]), [SWEBOK excerpt 2a7c513cf4547122d815])."*

---

**13. The write-up may include or be a formal specification, using rigorously defined notation that permits mechanical checking for consistency, completeness, and correctness — which is especially valuable when automated agents must follow the process.**

Formal methods reduce or eliminate the ambiguity that natural languages inherently carry. If coding agents must execute the workflow, a formally specified process can be checked automatically, reducing misinterpretation.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 7c862cc52c6bb02eff73]."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Development Methodologies and Approaches/Formal Methods in Software Engineering.md)

---

**14. The write-up may include behavioral models such as flowcharts, state-transition diagrams, sequence diagrams, or data-flow models, to represent the dynamic behaviour of the workflow.**

Behavioral models are especially suited to a hand-off workflow because they explicitly capture sequences, triggering events, and state changes — the very things that characterise passing work from one person to the next.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 10dc00009229117a1e0e] … Data flow diagrams … provide [SWEBOK excerpt 81807e39648ba24882de] elements."*

---

**15. The write-up may include methods and formalism from established process frameworks (e.g., Rational Unified Process, Extreme Programming), which bring pre-defined structure and terminology that practitioners already understand.**

Reusing a recognised framework means the write-up does not have to invent its structure from scratch and gives new developers a shared vocabulary they may already know.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 2777702c04be165ba800])."*
> — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Engineering Processes, Lifecycles, and Measurement/Software Process Foundations and Measurement/Software Process Infrastructure Tools and Notations.md)

**16. The write-up may take the form of texts, diagrams, models, or prototypes, and in practice should use multiple types of representation to serve different audiences and concerns.**

No single notation satisfies every reader. New developers may need prose; automated agents may need a formal model; a quick overview for stakeholders may need a diagram. Using several complementary forms is explicitly endorsed.
