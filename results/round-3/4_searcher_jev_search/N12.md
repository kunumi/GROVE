## Guidelines for Measuring Numerical Test Discrepancy

### 1. Replace the fixed absolute threshold with a relative-error check

Absolute error — `|x* − x|` — is scale-blind: the same threshold of 0.001 is simultaneously too tight for a result of 1 000 000 and too loose for a result of 0.0001. Use **relative error** instead, defined as `|x* − x| / |x|`, which scales automatically with the magnitude of the expected value.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt e34f7850b6927fbf9f5d]|" — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Foundations of Mathematics and Computational Systems/Numerical Methods, Algebra, and Calculus Foundations/Numerical Precision and Error Measurement/Precision, Absolute Error, and Relative Error.md`)

---

### 2. Prefer relative error as the primary measure of approximation quality

Relative error is not just mathematically valid; it is the intuitively preferred measure. Because it expresses error as a proportion of the true value, the same threshold (e.g., 10⁻⁶) is meaningful whether the values are tiny or enormous.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 297dadd84958fa3cc461]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Foundations of Mathematics and Computational Systems/Numerical Methods, Algebra, and Calculus Foundations/Numerical Precision and Error Measurement/Precision, Absolute Error, and Relative Error.md`)

---

### 3. Set the tolerance against the full expected magnitude, not an arbitrary constant

A concrete illustration from the SWEBOK makes the scale-dependence vivid: an approximation of 1 000 001 by 1 000 000 has absolute error 1 but relative error 10⁻⁶ (excellent), while an approximation of 11 by 10 also has absolute error 1 but relative error 0.1 (poor). A flat threshold of 0.001 would accept both equally, masking the fact that the second is roughly a million times worse in relative terms.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 3baaec5a616a729ec42c]⁻⁶, [SWEBOK excerpt 8f40bc3ffa6de1a9ad8f]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Foundations of Mathematics and Computational Systems/Numerical Methods, Algebra, and Calculus Foundations/Numerical Precision and Error Measurement/Precision, Absolute Error, and Relative Error.md`)

---

### 4. Use the relative-error threshold to set how many correct leading digits are required

The relative error directly encodes the number of trustworthy significant (leading) digits: **a computed value x\* has k correct significant decimal digits if and only if its relative error is less than 5 × 10⁻⁽ᵏ⁺¹⁾**. Choosing a tolerance of, say, 5 × 10⁻⁴ therefore accepts results with 3 correct significant digits; tightening to 5 × 10⁻⁷ requires 6 correct significant digits.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt e98f68a3670db66a99c0]⁻ᵏ⁻¹." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Foundations of Mathematics and Computational Systems/Numerical Methods, Algebra, and Calculus Foundations/Numerical Precision and Error Measurement/Precision, Absolute Error, and Relative Error.md`)

---

### 5. Interpret "significant digits" as the leading digits whose correctness is confirmed

Significant digits are the digits of a result that are **known to be correct**. The connection to measurement precision is explicit: a ruler reading of 15.5 mm (±0.5 mm) yields 2 significant digits, while a caliper reading of 15.47 mm (±0.01 mm) yields 3 significant digits. In a test, the chosen relative-error bound maps one-to-one to the count of leading digits the test is certifying as correct.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt f7f3a65aa84a447e276f]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Foundations of Mathematics and Computational Systems/Numerical Methods, Algebra, and Calculus Foundations/Significant Digits in Measurements.md`)

---

### 6. Fall back to absolute error only when the expected value is exactly zero

Relative error requires dividing by the expected value x, so it is **undefined when x = 0**. In that special case, the SWEBOK's framework provides no scale-independent alternative, so an absolute threshold remains the only option — but its value must be justified on domain grounds rather than treated as a universal constant.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 9074e2bd5aef274b7298]." — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Foundations of Mathematics and Computational Systems/Numerical Methods, Algebra, and Calculus Foundations/Numerical Precision and Error Measurement/Precision, Absolute Error, and Relative Error.md`)
