## Guidelines for Evaluating Test Suite Fault-Detection Quality (Beyond Code Coverage)

---

### 1. Use Fault Injection to Estimate How Many Real Faults the Suite Can Detect

Seed artificial faults into the SUT and measure how many your test suite discovers. The fraction detected gives an estimate of the suite's ability to uncover genuine faults, and it may also allow extrapolating the number of remaining real faults.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 243d9c57faf056f32a15]."*

⚠️ Apply this with care: injected faults may accidentally be left in the code, and statisticians question whether their distribution truly represents real faults.

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation.md`)

---

### 2. Compute the Mutation Score

Run mutation testing: generate many slightly modified versions of the SUT (mutants), each differing by a small syntactic change, then check what fraction your tests kill. The higher the ratio of killed mutants to total mutants, the more effective your suite.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 0a8c460ad2e19c35f9dc] underlying rationale (the **coupling effect**) is that tests catching simple syntactic faults will also catch more complex real faults.

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation.md`)

---

### 3. Write New Tests Specifically to Kill Surviving Mutants

Don't just measure — act on the result. When mutants survive, design test cases explicitly targeting them. This upgrades mutation testing from a passive measurement into an active test-design criterion.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 097685fa3e3ed16f599f]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation-Based and Usage-Based Testing Techniques/Mutation Testing as a Testing Criterion.md`)

---

### 4. Build Fault Models and Apply Fault-Based Testing Techniques

Classify the types of faults likely to occur (e.g., via Orthogonal Defect Classification) and devise test cases specifically aimed at each predefined fault category. This focuses testing effort on the fault types that actually matter for your SUT.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 711adc363f9e008ed174]. […] the orthogonal defect classification (ODC) [is] [SWEBOK excerpt 63c9a3e839b6a6f455f6]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Testing Approaches and Execution Strategies/Smoke Testing and Knowledge-Based Testing Approaches.md` and `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Fault Models and Mutation Testing Techniques.md`)

---

### 5. Apply Error Guessing Informed by Fault History

Draw on the history of faults found in earlier projects and on tester expertise to design test cases that anticipate the most plausible fault locations. Past fault data is a direct indicator of where future faults are likely to hide.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 58750920721c36886ebb]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Testing Approaches and Execution Strategies/Software Testing Techniques and Methodologies/Experience-Based Techniques for Test Suite Generation.md`)

---

### 6. Add Boundary Value Tests, Including Robustness Cases

Since many faults cluster near the extreme values of inputs, verify that your suite includes tests at and near boundaries. For fault-detection completeness, also add **robustness tests** — cases outside the input domain — to check how the SUT handles unexpected or erroneous inputs.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 22b5a76f3ce0e0a3a3af]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing Methodologies and Techniques/Boundary Value Analysis and Syntax Testing Techniques.md`)

---

### 7. Include Exception-Forcing Tests for Known Error Categories

Ensure your suite contains tests that deliberately trigger a predefined set of exception/error conditions (data exception, overflow, underflow, protection, operation exceptions). A suite that never exercises error-handling paths is blind to entire fault classes.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt ad9e5de651676947d75f])."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Testing Approaches and Execution Strategies/Software Testing Techniques and Methodologies/Exception Testing and Error Handling in Software Testing.md`)

---

### 8. Measure Fault Density and Track it Over Time

Count discovered faults relative to SUT size. A suite that consistently yields high fault density is exposing weaknesses that a low-density suite is missing. Also consider supplementary measures such as fault depth (minimum repairs needed to make the SUT correct) and fault multiplicity (number of atomic changes needed per repair).

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt fcd099864e112f4cbf75]. […] [SWEBOK excerpt 6d8ae81aaa9c80008958])."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Fault Density Measurement in Software Testing.md`)

---

### 9. Measure Relative Effectiveness by Comparing Techniques

Evaluate effectiveness not in isolation but relative to other techniques, using properties such as: how many tests were [SWEBOK excerpt fc34bb92c0fdd213fb95] faults found during testing versus all faults found (including post-release), and how much reliability improved.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt c4ad890b5668c6dfc1ec]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Test Effectiveness and Process Levels/Test Suite Effectiveness and Testing Process Integration.md`)

---

### 10. Assess Testability: Does the Suite Actually Expose Failures When Faults Exist?

Consider the statistical likelihood that the test suite will expose a failure when the software is faulty — this is one of the two formal meanings of **testability**. A suite with high coverage but low exposure probability is not truly effective.

> *"[Testability] [SWEBOK excerpt 38b9b258b6c18a604f02]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Limitations and Path Feasibility Challenges/Infeasible Paths, Testability, and Test Execution Automation.md`)

---

### 11. Use Data-Flow Testing Criteria to Check Variable Usage Faults

Augment structural coverage with data-flow criteria (All-Definitions, All-Uses, All-DU-Paths). These specifically target faults related to incorrect variable definition and use patterns that statement/branch coverage alone may miss.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt fd56de9cc0c87fbf5fd0]). […] [SWEBOK excerpt 26097448298091818c42] All-DU-[SWEBOK excerpt 0cc3c9229d49c80bfab5]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Testing Approaches and Execution Strategies/Software Testing Techniques and Methodologies/Data Flow Testing Techniques and Coverage Criteria.md`)

---

### 12. Prioritize Test Cases to Maximize the Rate of Fault Detection

Reorder your test suite so that the cases most likely to find faults run first. This maximizes fault detection rate early in execution and raises the probability of catching faults before testing is stopped.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 3e32889b1d11770ab3a7]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing Levels, Objectives, and Approaches/Test Case Prioritization for Fault Detection.md`)
