### Question

How should modules be organized so that the software stays easy to maintain, and how can an existing badly structured program be improved without its users noticing any difference?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
SWEBOK v4 teaches that good module organization requires minimizing complexity through modular design and simple readable code, using divide-and-conquer and stepwise refinement strategies to decompose functions systematically, anticipating change to keep modules extensible, and explicitly designing for non-runtime quality attributes such as modifiability and testability. When an existing system is badly structured, the prescribed approach is **refactoring** — a technique that reorganizes the program's internal structure and improves maintainability *without changing its behavior*, so users notice no difference; in CI/Agile contexts this should be done continuously to manage growing complexity.

# Guidelines

- **Minimize complexity via modular design and simple, readable code** — [SWEBOK excerpt 2a925e82b0ef7eebb1cf], using standards and modular design. (source: `root/Software Design and Construction Fundamentals/Software Construction and Code Complexity Management/Software Construction Tools and Anticipating Change.md`)

- **Apply divide-and-conquer and stepwise refinement strategies to structure modules** — these general strategies guide designers on [SWEBOK excerpt 2b427e77ae6c2c803297]. (source: `root/Software Design Methodologies and Approaches/Design Strategies and Structured Approaches/Design Strategies and Function-Oriented Methods.md`)

- **Decompose functions top-down in structured design** — structured design focuses on refinement/[SWEBOK excerpt 941beb18987d775abe74]. (source: `root/Software Design Methodologies and Approaches/Design Strategies and Structured Approaches/Structured Design and Top-Down Function Decomposition.md`)

- **Anticipate and embrace change when defining module boundaries** — [SWEBOK excerpt 3005c582c73eababb53e] product without disrupting the underlying structure. (source: `root/Software Design and Construction Fundamentals/Software Construction and Code Complexity Management/Software Construction Tools and Anticipating Change.md`)

- **Design explicitly for modifiability, portability, reusability, and testability** — these quality attributes cannot be observed at runtime and must be addressed in the design itself. (source: `root/Software Design Quality Evaluation and Assessment/Software Quality Analysis and Evaluation Techniques.md`)

- **Review and statically analyze module structure to find weaknesses** — architecture reviews, design reviews, inspections, scenario-based techniques, and formal static analysis can be used to evaluate design quality. (source: `root/Software Design Quality Evaluation and Assessment/Software Quality Analysis and Evaluation Techniques.md`)

- **Record design rationale to keep the module structure understandable over time** — recording the rationale (assumptions, alternatives, trade-offs) enhances long-term maintainability; continuing to capture it during maintenance also contributes to viability. (source: `root/Software Design Concerns, Documentation, and Modeling/Recording Design Rationale for System Maintainability.md`)

- **Refactor: reorganize the program's internals without changing its behavior** — [SWEBOK excerpt 2b93c402ca4e17e31a18], improving internal structure and maintainability; it can reduce technical debt before and after code changes. (source: `root/Software Maintenance and Configuration Management/Software Maintenance, Evolution, and Quality Assurance/Refactoring and Reverse Engineering in Software Development.md`)

- **Refactor continuously in CI/Agile contexts to manage growing complexity** — [SWEBOK excerpt bb2ba03a0a8e6f9e09c9] reducing and managing growing complexity while improving developer productivity. (source: `root/Software Maintenance and Configuration Management/Software Maintenance, Evolution, and Quality Assurance/Refactoring and Reverse Engineering in Software Development.md`)

- **Use reverse engineering to understand the existing structure before restructuring** — reverse engineering identifies components and their interrelationships and creates representations at higher levels of abstraction, enabling informed refactoring. (source: `root/Software Maintenance and Configuration Management/Software Maintenance, Evolution, and Quality Assurance/Refactoring and Reverse Engineering in Software Development.md`)

- **Apply core construction fundamentals when restructuring** — minimizing complexity, anticipating change, constructing for verification, reusing assets, and applying standards all apply to redesign work, not just initial construction. (source: `root/Software Design and Construction Fundamentals/Software Construction and Code Complexity Management/Software Construction Fundamentals and Practices/Software Construction Fundamentals.md`)
