### Question

I am handing over a finished system to a client whose in-house team will keep developing it after delivery. Besides the program itself, what written material should I prepare for the client, for its end users and for my own organization, and for how long should it be retained?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
At system handover, you should prepare a **Concept Document** and a **Software Maintenance Plan** (covering scope, processes, tools, and how users report problems), a **Software Configuration Management Plan (SCMP)** as a living reference, **design work products and design-rationale records** (assumptions, alternatives, trade-offs), **configuration status accounting records** (approved CIs, change status, V&V evidence, deviations/waivers), and **SLA/contract documentation**. End users need a documented problem-reporting and help-desk process, plus release/version information. All active documentation must be kept current until the software is retired from service—no fixed number of years is stated, but the BoK notes that "[SWEBOK excerpt b0dc290020596123f7b3]."

# Guidelines

- **Prepare a Concept Document before writing the Maintenance Plan.** Planning should begin during development; a concept document is developed first, followed by a maintenance plan that addresses quality objectives. (source: `root/Software Maintenance Planning and Supporting Activities.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt cbd1c5b60252ab421681].")

- **Deliver a Software Maintenance Plan specifying [SWEBOK excerpt 2a01d0619f3b482a5266].** It must cover business planning, release/version planning, and individual MR planning. (source: `root/Software Maintenance and Configuration Management/.../Software Maintenance Planning and Cost Estimation.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt dcd77385d2336dc761c2].")

- **Hand over a Software Configuration Management Plan (SCMP) as a living document.** It must include sections on Introduction, SCM Management (organization, responsibilities, authorities, policies), SCM Activities, SCM Schedules, SCM Resources, and SCMP Maintenance. (source: `root/Software Maintenance and Configuration Management/.../Software Configuration Management Plan Development.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt c695a232ef1c7f32810f].")

- **Record all design work products:** the vocabulary of the problem domain, the solution vocabulary, the major decisions taken, and the rationale for each non-trivial decision. These help [SWEBOK excerpt 93fd1c443e83343c035f]. (source: `root/Software Design Concerns, Documentation, and Modeling/Software Design Documentation and Communication Methods/Recording Software Designs.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 858758e2ff2eff169624]…; ([SWEBOK excerpt 59eaedf69004d1128ce1].")

- **Document design rationale explicitly:** record prior assumptions, alternatives considered, and trade-offs used to select one approach over others. Continuing to capture [SWEBOK excerpt 450c2ce0af724fbb8217]. (source: `root/Software Design Concerns, Documentation, and Modeling/Recording Design Rationale for System Maintainability.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 1ab89a13846ac4547d32] long-term [SWEBOK excerpt 4055d4b878dcd6f73d39].")

- **Provide Configuration Status Accounting records** covering [SWEBOK excerpt ef5a2614e0ebab57c45e], deviations and waivers, and V&V activities — usable by the development team, operations, maintenance team, security, and project management. (source: `root/.../Software Configuration Status Accounting and Reporting.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 612a48dbf73aead9e586].")

- **Provide SCM audit evidence** so the client can demonstrate compliance: SCM procedures should cover verification, validation, and audit of each step [SWEBOK excerpt 6be8b2e7d3f428004d39] and its IT assets; status reports can serve as evidence of regulatory and governance compliance. (source: `root/.../Software Configuration Status Reporting and Auditing.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt cc6a64cbb430fd68a2f6].")

- **For end users, document the help-desk and problem-reporting process.** The help desk is the end-user and maintenance-coordinated function that triggers assessment, prioritization, and costing of modification requests and incidents. (source: `root/Software Maintenance Costs, Metrics, and Quality/Software Maintenance Processes and Activities.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt e25a23005ce83fc4e7d8].")

- **Keep end users informed of release/version plans.** The maintainer must collect availability dates for [SWEBOK excerpt 8ee22aeedcc2887f72d2], and inform all stakeholders. (source: `root/Software Maintenance Planning and Supporting Activities.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt b57992f63bc0045304ca]… Inform all stakeholders.")

- **For your own organization, retain documentation of all ongoing support activities** including SCM, V&V, problem resolution, SQA, reviews, vulnerability assessments, audits, and SCM status reports produced to meet security, legal, or regulatory requirements. (source: `root/Software Maintenance Planning and Supporting Activities.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt addeaef604bd87ceebf9].")

- **Document and retain all SLAs and maintenance contracts.** Maintenance service-level indicators (SLIs), SLOs, SLAs, and software/hardware licenses and contracts are contractual agreements describing [SWEBOK excerpt 8903875c697ec1cf4e73]. (source: `root/Software Maintenance Planning and Supporting Activities.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 7413dc50725b83b67e5b].")

- **Retain all active documentation until the software is retired.** The maintenance phase [SWEBOK excerpt e5aabbce3fff8efbd15d], so all plans and the SCMP must remain current throughout that entire span. (source: `root/Software Maintenance Planning and Supporting Activities.md` — "[SWEBOK excerpt 36af09d147767eb06737].")

# Gaps
- No specific numeric retention period (e.g., a fixed number of years after retirement) is prescribed in the BoK; it only links retention to the active maintenance lifecycle ending at software disposal.
- No explicit guidance on user manuals, training materials, or operational handbooks for end users (beyond the help-desk/problem-reporting process and release notifications).
- No explicit description of what documentation the *delivering* organization retains solely for its own post-handover records, separate from what it transfers to the client.
