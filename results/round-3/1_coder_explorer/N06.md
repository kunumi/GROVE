### Question

At the end of each release our bug tracker tells us only how many bugs were found, and that count gives the team nothing to act on. What should we do with our bug records so that they help us introduce fewer bugs in later releases?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
Raw bug counts give no actionable information; SWEBOK v4 prescribes enriching each bug record with type/classification data, performing root cause analysis to find underlying causes, applying statistical and graphical techniques (Pareto, control charts, trend analysis) to understand which defect types dominate, and feeding the resulting defect profiles and lessons learned back into a continuous improvement cycle (PDCA/Kaizen) and the organization's measurement database so future projects can focus quality effort where problems are most likely to recur.

(source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Quality Management and Defect Classification.md` — *"[SWEBOK excerpt 5cd5d7028e5f2b502a89]"*)
