### Question

Beyond code coverage, how can I evaluate whether my test suite is good at detecting faults?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
SWEBOK v4 describes multiple ways to evaluate test suite fault-detection effectiveness beyond code coverage. The core techniques are **mutation testing** (measuring what fraction of artificially-introduced syntactic faults your tests can "kill"), **fault injection** (introducing artificial faults and counting how many are revealed), and **reliability growth models** (inferring quality from observed failure trends). These are complemented by fault density counts, usage-based/operational-profile testing, and comparative effectiveness studies across different test techniques.

# Guidelines

- **Use mutation testing as your primary evaluation technique.** Create mutants — slightly modified versions of the SUT differing by a small syntactic change — and execute the test suite against them. If a test detects the difference, the mutant is "killed." The underlying assumption (the *coupling effect*) is that tests that catch simple syntactic faults will also catch more complex real faults. [SWEBOK excerpt 8cab7755827c571b8145].
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Fault Models and Mutation Testing Techniques.md`)

- **Compute the mutation score** — the ratio of killed mutants to the total number of generated mutants. The higher the score, the stronger the test suite's ability to discover real faults.
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation.md`)

- **Design new tests specifically to kill surviving mutants.** When mutants survive, it reveals gaps. Tests can be designed to target those gaps, making mutation testing also a structure-based criterion. It has also been used effectively for generating fuzz tests.
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation-Based and Usage-Based Testing Techniques/Mutation Testing as a Testing Criterion.md`)

- **Apply fault injection** — artificially introducing faults before testing to estimate how many the test suite reveals and to infer the remaining number of genuine faults. Use with care: injected faults may be unrepresentative of real faults, and there is a risk of leaving them in the SUT.
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation.md`)

- **Measure fault density** — count discovered faults as a ratio of faults found to SUT size. Supplement with *fault depth* (minimum removals to make SUT correct) and *fault multiplicity* ([SWEBOK excerpt b9f3477d4f96f3fae60b]).
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Fault Density Measurement in Software Testing.md`)

- **Study historical fault classifications and frequencies** to focus testing on fault types most likely to occur. Use methodologies like Orthogonal Defect Classification (ODC) to collect semantic information and reduce root-cause analysis effort.
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/SUT Measurements and Fault Classification for Test Planning.md`)

- **Apply reliability growth models** — predict and track reliability improvement based on observed failures over successive test cycles. When faults causing failures are fixed, reliability should measurably grow; slower growth signals a weaker test suite.
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Reliability Growth Models and Test Evaluation.md`)

- **Test against an operational profile** — ensure the test suite reflects realistic usage patterns. Statistical sampling simulates many usage scenarios and allows inference of future reliability from observed test results.
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Operational Profile Testing for Software Reliability.md` and `Mutation-Based and Usage-Based Testing Techniques/Usage-Based Testing Techniques and Statistical Sampling.md`)

- **Compare relative effectiveness across techniques** using properties such as: [SWEBOK excerpt 54d4dba195fa7bc08201], ratio of faults found during testing to all faults found during and after testing, and amount of reliability improvement achieved.
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Test Effectiveness and Process Levels/Test Suite Effectiveness and Testing Process Integration.md`)

- **Apply multi-viewpoint coverage measures** — beyond branch/statement coverage, evaluate the percentage of functional requirements exercised, using both structure-based and specification-based measures to assess both thoroughness and fault-revealing effectiveness.
  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Test Measurement and Coverage Analysis/Coverage Measures in Software Testing Techniques.md`)
