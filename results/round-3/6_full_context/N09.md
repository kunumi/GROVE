# Guidelines for Choosing Among Three Candidate Libraries with Mixed Tangible and Intangible Criteria

## 1. Recognize this as a multiple-attribute decision, not a purely financial one

When criteria beyond money are relevant and cannot all be cast in monetary terms, use multiple-attribute decision-making techniques that allow non-monetary criteria to be factored into the decision alongside financial ones. License cost is one criterion; performance, maturity, community activity, and team familiarity are others.

## 3. Explicitly surface and characterize the intangible assets involved

Team familiarity, community health, and library maturity are intangible assets — knowledge assets that affect financial performance but have no physical substance. Use a structured approach to identify them, assess their quality and their impact on your business goals, and characterize their current state before making a decision.

## 5. Measure or estimate each criterion on an appropriate scale — and respect what that scale allows

Apply measurement theory: nominal, ordinal, interval, and ratio scales each support different operations. Performance benchmarks may be ratio-scale; team familiarity or community activity may only be ordinal. Combining them arithmetically without accounting for scale type produces meaningless results and can easily lead to erroneous conclusions.

(source: Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Ordinal and Interval Scales in Measurement.md)
