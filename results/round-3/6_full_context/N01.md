### Question

A feature request I have to implement is written as loose prose, and the product owner and I keep reading it differently. How should the team pin down, together with the domain experts and before any code is written, what the feature must do, so that it cannot be misread?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

Here are guidelines for pinning down feature requirements before coding begins, so they cannot be misread:

1. **Perform structured elicitation with all relevant stakeholder classes, not just the product owner.** Identify clients, users, subject matter experts (SMEs), and operations staff. Working in terms of stakeholder classes rather than individuals can produce important additional insight, and reduces the risk that requirements are biased toward better-represented stakeholders.
(source: Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Software Requirements Engineering Fundamentals/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Stakeholder Identification and Requirements Elicitation Techniques/Stakeholders and Requirements Elicitation/Software Project Stakeholders and Their Roles.md)

2. **Apply the "5-whys" technique to converge on the true problem behind the loose prose.** When an elicited statement describes a solution rather than a real need, repeatedly ask "Why is this the requirement?" until the stakeholder's actual problem is exposed. [SWEBOK excerpt 4ddc320ca31e89ad7143]."
(source: Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md)

3. **Rewrite each requirement so it is unambiguous, testable (quantified), atomic, and uses stakeholder vocabulary.** Each requirement should be interpretable in only one way and demonstrate compliance or noncompliance clearly. The overall set must be internally consistent, with no requirement conflicting with any other.
(source: Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md)

4. **Apply the Perfect Technology Filter to separate functional from nonfunctional requirements.** [SWEBOK excerpt d0b1c1c03de365829ce3] perfect computer existed. This prevents technical constraints from being entangled with behavioral requirements, allowing domain experts to review only what is relevant to them.
(source: Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Software Requirements Engineering Fundamentals/Functional and Nonfunctional Requirements Classification.md)

5. **Use Acceptance Test-Driven Development (ATDD) or Behavior-Driven Development (BDD) to express requirements as precise test-case scenarios before any production code is written.** In ATDD, engineers, business domain experts, and QA professionals meet before any design or construction work to agree on test cases that must pass. 6. **If using BDD, write full "Given / When / Then" scenarios covering all significant alternate and exception paths.** For example, in addition to the happy-path scenario, write separate scenarios for each exception condition. (source: Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Test-Driven Development and Requirements Specification/Acceptance Criteria and BDD Scenarios for Banking Systems.md)
