### Answer

Here are guidelines for evaluating whether your test suite is good at detecting faults, drawn from the SWEBOK:

---

**1. Use mutation testing to measure how many injected mutants your test suite can kill.**

Mutation testing generates slightly modified versions of the system under test (mutants), and the ratio of killed mutants to total generated mutants gives a mutation score. A higher score indicates a stronger ability to discover real faults. (source: `Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation-Based and Usage-Based Testing Techniques/Mutation Testing as a Testing Criterion.md`)

---

**2. Use fault injection to estimate how many real faults remain undetected.**

Artificially injected faults are run through the test suite; the proportion discovered gives an indication of testing effectiveness, and the remaining genuine fault count can be estimated. Be aware that statisticians question the representativeness of injected faults relative to genuine ones. (source: `Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation.md`)
