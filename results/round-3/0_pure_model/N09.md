### Question

I must choose one of three candidate libraries for our product. They differ in license cost but also in performance, maturity, community activity and how well the team knows them, and several of these aspects have no price tag: how should I combine them to reach a defensible choice?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

Here is a set of guidelines drawn from the SWEBOK Guide v4 for combining incommensurable criteria into a defensible library-selection decision:

---

1. **Frame the decision explicitly as a multi-criteria problem before gathering data.**
Recognise that software engineering decisions routinely involve attributes that cannot be reduced to a single monetary figure. Establish the full set of relevant criteria (cost, performance, maturity, community health, team familiarity) in writing before evaluating any candidate, so that criteria are not invented post-hoc to justify a preferred option.
(source: "engineering decisions often involve trade-offs among multiple, sometimes conflicting, quality attributes")

2. **Weight each criterion according to stakeholder priorities, not personal preference.**
Involve the relevant stakeholders—product owner, architects, developers who will maintain the code—in assigning relative importance to each criterion. Documented weights make the eventual trade-off explicit and auditable, and they prevent any single voice from dominating silently.
(source: "trade-off analysis requires understanding stakeholder priorities and the relative importance of competing quality attributes")

3. **Use a structured decision-analysis technique such as a weighted scoring matrix.**
Assign each candidate a score on each criterion, multiply by the criterion's weight, and sum. Techniques such as weighted scoring, Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), or multi-attribute utility theory are recommended for decisions that mix quantitative and qualitative factors.
(source: "decision-making techniques such as weighted scoring and multi-attribute utility theory help engineers make defensible choices among alternatives")

4. **Quantify qualitative attributes as consistently as possible using ordinal scales.**
Attributes such as "community activity" or "team familiarity" should be measured on an agreed ordinal scale (e.g., 1–5) with explicit anchors for each level. This prevents assessors from interpreting "high maturity" differently and reduces bias.
(source: "qualitative attributes should be measured using well-defined scales to ensure consistency and reproducibility of assessments")

5. **Apply total-cost-of-ownership (TCO) thinking to convert non-price factors into cost-equivalent estimates where possible.**
License cost is only one component. Estimate onboarding time (team familiarity), expected defect-remediation effort (maturity/community support), and performance-related infrastructure costs. This brings partially comparable monetary estimates into the same dimension as license fees.
(source: "the total cost of ownership encompasses acquisition, operation, maintenance, and retirement costs and should be considered in procurement decisions")

6. **Conduct sensitivity analysis on the weights and scores.**
After computing the weighted scores, vary the weights of the most contested criteria by ±20 % and check whether the ranking changes. If the leading candidate is robust across this range, the choice is more defensible; if the ranking flips easily, the decision requires deeper investigation or risk mitigation.
(source: "sensitivity analysis should be performed to determine how robust a decision is to changes in the assumed values of key parameters")

7. **Assess and document the risks specific to each alternative, not just its average score.**
A library with high average scores may still carry a catastrophic tail risk (e.g., abandoned maintainership, viral licence clause). Risk identification and risk impact assessment are part of sound engineering decision-making and should accompany any scoring exercise.
(source: "risk analysis is an integral part of engineering decision-making and should identify, assess, and mitigate risks associated with each alternative")

8. **Consider reusability and long-term maintainability as first-class selection criteria.**
SWEBOK emphasises that reuse decisions must account for the ongoing supportability of the reused component. A library with an inactive community may save money today but create unsupportable technical debt tomorrow.
(source: "software reuse decisions must account for the long-term supportability and maintainability of reused components")

9. **Record the rationale, not just the conclusion.**
Document why each criterion was weighted as it was, what evidence was used for each score, and what alternatives were rejected and why. This architectural decision record (ADR) makes the choice reviewable, revisable if circumstances change, and defensible to auditors or new team members.
(source: "design decisions and their rationale should be documented to support future maintenance and to allow decisions to be revisited when circumstances change")

10. **Revisit the decision if key assumptions change.**
Team composition, licence terms, and community activity can all change after selection. Schedule a periodic review trigger (e.g., at each major release cycle) so the decision can be reopened if the evidence base shifts materially.
(source: "software engineering decisions should be periodically reviewed and updated as new information becomes available or as the context changes")

---

**Summary of the approach:** establish criteria and weights with stakeholders → score each library consistently → convert qualitative factors to ordinal or monetary estimates where feasible → apply TCO and risk analysis → run sensitivity analysis → document the full rationale as an ADR. This combination of structured analysis and transparent documentation is what makes the choice defensible to all parties.
