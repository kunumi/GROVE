## Guidelines for Choosing Among Candidate Libraries Using Multiple Criteria

### 1. Treat the Decision as a Multiple-Attribute Problem from the Start
Money (license cost) is usually the most important single criterion, but it is not always the only one. Apply multiple-attribute decision-making techniques that allow non-monetary criteria — such as performance, maturity, and team familiarity — to be formally factored into the decision alongside cost.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt b4a03fe3cebc8941c34c]."*)

---

### 2. Define Selection Criteria Explicitly — but Keep the Number Manageable
List all relevant criteria before evaluating any library. Too many criteria make the analysis unwieldy; too few may not differentiate the candidates and can lead to a suboptimal choice.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt ad779e7ae7316b9713b2]."*)

---

### 3. Name and Record the Non-Monetary Criteria as Intangibles/Unquantifiables
Criteria like community activity, maturity, and team know-how that cannot be expressed as a monetary value are called "unquantifiables," "irreducibles," or "intangibles." Acknowledge them by name, because ignoring them risks a solution that does not fit the organisation.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt a4318de5004b25042ec1].'"*)

---

### 4. Explicitly Surface and Weigh the Team's Tacit Knowledge as an Asset
Team familiarity with a library is an intangible asset — specifically a form of tacit/implicit [SWEBOK excerpt b1cd5126984578065b38]." They must be explicitly considered; if they are not, software engineers risk a solution that does not fit the organisation.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 5eed27bb7bc8930d99e2]… [SWEBOK excerpt 3251be727c50fd405088]."*)

### 5. Use a Compensatory Technique to Combine All Criteria into One Ranking
Apply a **compensatory** technique — such as **additive weighting** or the **Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)** — to [SWEBOK excerpt 6ed292052877d4475a99]. These techniques allow a lower score on one criterion (e.g., higher licence cost) to be traded off against a higher score on another (e.g., better performance or greater team familiarity).

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt dd90612de75ae5a7ee4c])."*)

---

### 6. Alternatively, Use a Non-Compensatory Technique if Any Criterion Is a Hard Threshold
If any criterion is a true knock-out condition (e.g., the licence must not exceed a fixed budget, or the library must have an OSI-approved licence), consider a **non-compensatory** technique such as **satisficing** or **dominance**. These techniques treat each criterion separately and do not allow trade-offs across criteria.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 0aef8ab359ad61f5d97e]. Evaluate All Alternatives Against Each Criterion on the Same Basis
When a criterion involves money, use the same planning horizon and the same kinds of costs (acquisition, maintenance, training, customisation) for every library. Comparing one library's cost over a short horizon against another's over a longer one will make the shorter-horizon option look artificially better.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 52782f508d83e0a6b786]… [SWEBOK excerpt 8c6be2eafa754bc08485]."*)

### 8. Plan for the Total Cost of Ownership, Including Training and Customisation
When assessing licence cost, do not stop at the initial price. For each candidate library, ask explicitly who will pay for acquisition, maintenance, training, and customisation — and over what scope the tool will be deployed.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 5d85a9c813071b1cba33]?"*)

### 9. For Open-Source Options, Review Licence Restrictions Carefully
An open-source library may be "nominally free," but the organisation may still need to [SWEBOK excerpt 13e1a5005dab718c48cc] licence for restrictions on use — costs and constraints that must feed into the evaluation.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 991ef6bbbff3030dc046]"*)

### 10. Use Prototyping and Peer Review to Verify Technical Feasibility Before Committing
Before finalising weights and scores, run small proofs-of-concept (throwaway prototypes) with the leading candidates. Peer review can also verify technical feasibility — especially for performance claims — and may reveal additional candidates.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 83a16aaaef38b6fc38f4]."*)

---

### 11. Assign a Qualitative Importance Weight to Each Intangible Criterion
For criteria that cannot be directly quantified (community activity, maturity, team familiarity), assign a relative importance value — for example, on a 1–5 scale — based on how strongly each one supports achieving the project's business goals. ### 12. Consider All Dimensions Together — There Is No Simple Single Rule
When cost, time to integrate, complexity, impact on business goals, and intangible dimensions all matter, there is no single shortcut rule. All criteria must be considered together, making this explicitly a multiple-attribute decision.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 6a3437396dcb8f7ca0aa]… [SWEBOK excerpt 7a0dc59443a621c49566]."*)

---

### 13. Perform a Sensitivity Analysis to Test How Robust the Choice Is
Engineering decisions rest on estimates that have limited accuracy. Run a sensitivity analysis — varying the weight of uncertain criteria (e.g., how much the team's familiarity really matters) — to check whether the preferred library remains the winner across plausible ranges. If the ranking flips, that uncertainty should trigger either more investigation or delayed final commitment.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 4b2f6ebfdcf3c83c2b23]… [SWEBOK excerpt 89fe88682dd0f65a7b5f]."*)

---

### 14. Document the Decision as a Business Case
Prepare a business case — a consolidated record of the recommended choice from cost, benefit, and risk perspectives — so that decision-makers and stakeholders can follow the reasoning and the selection remains defensible over time.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 6f39885f3634c650d228]."*)

---

### 15. Close the Loop: Monitor the Chosen Library's Performance After Adoption
Engineering decision-making rests on estimates. After adoption, compare the estimates (performance, team ramp-up time, integration effort) with actual outcomes. This "closes the loop," reveals where estimates were wrong, and improves future decisions.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 0a22155f16372db877ef]. This also helps improve estimation over time."*)
