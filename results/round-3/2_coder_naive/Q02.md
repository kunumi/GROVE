## The Approach: Table-Driven Methods

The construction technique that directly addresses a long, hard-to-change chain of conditionals is the **table-driven method**. SWEBOK describes it and specifies exactly what must be decided when applying it. Here are the guidelines:

---

### 1. Recognise when a table-driven method is appropriate and prefer it over logic statements

> "[SWEBOK excerpt 37084ddc7c80ea4b579b]`)."

Whenever a function is dominated by `if`/`case` chains that map conditions to results, a table-driven approach is a candidate replacement.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 8f07d1cee310657857df])."* — SWEBOK v4, §[SWEBOK excerpt d894fc3e8c9a9fd89734]])

---

### 2. Apply the method only in appropriate circumstances — it is not universal

> "[SWEBOK excerpt 1f02a9a5cf54055acd0d]."

The method has real benefits, but only when the problem structure suits a tabular representation. Forcing it on the wrong problem can add unnecessary indirection.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 1f02a9a5cf54055acd0d]."* — SWEBOK v4, §4.7 [1-c18])

---

### 3. Decide what information to store in the table (the content decision)

> "[SWEBOK excerpt 68e8b2103379df006823]."

The first mandatory design decision is determining which data — values, actions, flags, function pointers, etc. — will live in the table. This replaces the conditional branches; each row or cell must capture exactly what a corresponding `if`/`case` branch would have encoded.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 68e8b2103379df006823]."* — SWEBOK v4, §4.7 [1-c18])

---

### 4. Decide [SWEBOK excerpt ffd30c20bbf41330ba5d] (the lookup decision)

The second mandatory design decision is the access strategy — direct indexing, stair-step lookup, or indexed lookup — to translate the runtime conditions into the correct table entry without reintroducing complicated logic.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 68e8b2103379df006823]."* — SWEBOK v4, §4.7 [1-c18])

---

### 5. Use the resulting simplicity to support modifiability and reduced complexity

SWEBOK links the goal of minimising complexity directly to the goal of modifiability:

> "[SWEBOK excerpt 5d5104b4c354f57e5286]."

A table-driven replacement for a conditional chain shortens the code, makes the mapping of conditions to results visible at a glance, and means that adding or changing a case requires only editing a table row rather than finding and altering intertwined logic.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 5d5104b4c354f57e5286]."* — SWEBOK v4, §1.1 Minimising Complexity [1, c2, c3, …])

---
