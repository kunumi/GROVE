### Question

A feature request I have to implement is written as loose prose, and the product owner and I keep reading it differently. How should the team pin down, together with the domain experts and before any code is written, what the feature must do, so that it cannot be misread?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

Here are guidelines for pinning down a feature's requirements unambiguously before coding begins:

1. **Conduct a structured requirements elicitation session with all relevant stakeholders.** Bring together clients, users, domain subject-matter experts, and developers in a facilitated workshop (e.g., a Joint Application Development or Joint Requirements Planning session) to surface the full, true requirements before any design or construction work starts. (source: `Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Stakeholder Identification and Requirements Elicitation Techniques/Stakeholder Elicitation Techniques and Requirements Sources.md`)

2. **Perform a stakeholder analysis to identify every class of stakeholder.** Different stakeholder classes have different perspectives and needs; missing a class means the requirements may be [SWEBOK excerpt 12da53720528e4668281] ones. (source: `Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Stakeholder Identification and Requirements Elicitation Techniques/Stakeholders and Requirements Elicitation/Stakeholder Analysis for Balanced Requirements.md`)

3. **Apply the 5-whys technique to reach the true underlying requirement.** When an elicited statement describes a solution rather than a problem, repeatedly ask "Why is this the requirement?" [SWEBOK excerpt 8717f3558b57f88d5e15]." (source: `Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md`)

4. **Verify that every individual requirement is unambiguous, testable, binding, and atomic.** Each requirement should be interpretable in only one way, quantified so compliance can be clearly demonstrated, agreed to be necessary by clients, and represent a single decision. (source: `Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md`)

5. **Specify the feature using acceptance-criteria-based language (ATDD or BDD).** Before any production design or construction, hold a meeting of software engineers, domain experts, and optionally QA professionals [SWEBOK excerpt 034474d2db7a7ebcc9a5] the feature is correctly implemented. Test-case language is very precise and directly addresses the ambiguity problem inherent in natural language. (source: `Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Test-Driven Development and Requirements Specification/Test Cases, Requirements, and Behavior-Driven Development.md`)

6. **Write BDD scenarios in the "Given / When / Then" form.** Express the feature as a user story ("[SWEBOK excerpt c6d4817748ea3e83ecf7]\>") and derive a comprehensive set of scenarios including typical flows, alternate flows, and error/boundary cases. These scenarios make acceptance criteria obvious and unambiguous to domain experts and developers alike. (source: `Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Test-Driven Development and Requirements Specification/Test Cases, Requirements, and Behavior-Driven Development.md`)

7. **Complement prose with model-based specifications to eliminate natural-language ambiguity.** Use selected elements of UML or SysML—for example, a logical class model for policies to be enforced, or state/sequence diagrams for processes to be carried out—in a computing-technology-free manner so that the feature can be precisely and concisely specified. The more formal the model, the less likely engineers are to misinterpret the requirements. (source: `Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Behavioral Models and Requirements Documentation/Formal Requirements Models and Additional Attributes.md`)

8. **Validate the written requirements with a multi-perspective review.** Have clients/users check that their needs are completely and accurately represented; have requirements-specialist engineers [SWEBOK excerpt c6abf9caec499940a7b9] standards; and have the architects or developers who will implement the feature [SWEBOK excerpt 3029ae1319171a4f6a92]. Provide reviewers with a checklist or "definition of done." (source: `Requirements Validation and Management/Requirements Validation Methods and Techniques.md`)

9. **Execute or simulate the specification with stakeholders before coding.** If the requirements are expressed in a model-based form, hand-interpret and "execute" the specification against a sufficient set of demonstration scenarios so that nontechnical stakeholders can confirm [SWEBOK excerpt 0487ce645ac66394d328]. (source: `Requirements Validation and Management/Requirements Validation Methods and Techniques.md`)

10. **Prototype only to validate assumptions, not to build the final product.** If the specification cannot be directly simulated, build a narrow prototype that concretely demonstrates the software engineer's interpretation of the requirements. This exposes hidden assumptions and gives domain experts concrete feedback on what the feature will actually do. (source: `Requirements Validation and Management/Prototyping for Requirements Validation and Feedback.md`)

11. **Document additional attributes—rationale, source, acceptance criteria, and dependencies—alongside each requirement.** Recording who imposed the requirement, why it matters, what the acceptance criteria are, and what it conflicts with or depends on gives the whole team shared context and prevents divergent interpretations later. (source: `Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Behavioral Models and Requirements Documentation/Formal Requirements Models and Additional Attributes.md`)

12. **Place the agreed requirements under configuration management immediately.** [SWEBOK excerpt b6c788720c5e49795a37] other life-cycle deliverables, so that any subsequent change is tracked, approved, and communicated to all affected stakeholders rather than drifting silently. (source: `Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Configuration Management and Requirements Specification Techniques.md`)
