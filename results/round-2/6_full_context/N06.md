### Question

At the end of each release our bug tracker tells us only how many bugs were found, and that count gives the team nothing to act on. What should we do with our bug records so that they help us introduce fewer bugs in later releases?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

Here are guidelines for making bug records actionable in future releases:

1. **Establish a meaningful defect classification taxonomy.** Don't just count bugs—categorize them by type, severity, and origin. Numbers alone, without classification, are insufficient to help identify underlying causes and prevent recurrence. (source: Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/Software Quality Management and Defect Classification.md)

2. **Perform root cause analysis (RCA) on defects.** Use techniques such as the 5-whys, cause-and-effect (Ishikawa) diagrams, or fault tree analysis to identify *why* bugs were introduced, not just that they occurred. (source: Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Standards, Quality Assurance, and Modern Manufacturing Systems/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Application/Root Cause Analysis Techniques.md)

3. **Track error density, defect density, and failure rate across releases.** Compute these as ratios (e.g., defects per unit size) to give the data analytical meaning and to compare trends over time. (source: Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/Quality Metrics, Reliability Models, and Corrective Actions/Software Quality Measurements and Reliability Models.md)

4. **Build defect profiles for your application domain.** Accumulate defect data across projects so that future projects can focus testing and review effort where problems are most likely to occur. Benchmarks—[SWEBOK excerpt 1fadfcbb81ceece9a90e]. (source: Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/Quality Metrics, Reliability Models, and Corrective Actions/Software Quality Measurements and Reliability Models.md)

5. **Raise formal corrective and preventive actions when quality objectives are not met.** Document them and monitor their progress so that the same type of defect does not recur in future projects. (source: Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/Quality Metrics, Reliability Models, and Corrective Actions/Corrective and Preventive Actions in Quality Management.md)

6. **Use defect type and frequency data to guide process improvement.** Feed the aggregated data back into the development process to change practices, tools, or standards that are generating recurring bug categories. [SWEBOK excerpt 153e2e04dd0ec4b37a72]. (source: Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/Quality Metrics, Reliability Models, and Corrective Actions/Software Quality Measurements and Reliability Models.md)

7. **Apply statistical analysis techniques to the defect data.** Use tools such as Pareto analysis, control charts, and trend analysis to visualize which defect categories dominate and to detect whether the process is improving or degrading over time. (source: Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Measurement and Analysis Techniques/Software Quality Measurement and Cost Analysis Techniques.md)

8. **Link defect records to the work products (requirements, design, code) in which the defects originated.** Traceability enables change analysis and helps identify which lifecycle phase is introducing the most defects, so earlier prevention activities can be targeted there. (source: Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Development Methods/Requirements Traceability and Artifact Management/Analyzing for Traceability in Software Development.md)

9. **Record defect data in a persistent organizational repository and mine it for estimation and planning.** Historical defect data should feed cost estimation models and risk registers for future releases; the data is only useful if it is stored and retrievable. [SWEBOK excerpt fa3f6e35a2adb0256fde]. (source: Software Maintenance Costs, Metrics, and Quality/Facility Rental and Maintenance Cost Estimation.md)

10. **Integrate defect tracking into the configuration management system.** Each change request should be traceable from the original defect report through its resolution, so that patterns of change-induced defects can also be analyzed.
