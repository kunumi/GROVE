## Guidelines for Combining Monetary and Non-Monetary Criteria to Choose Among Three Candidate Libraries

---

### 1. Recognise that this is a multiple-attribute decision from the outset

Money is usually important, but it is not always the only criterion. When other criteria cannot be expressed in monetary terms, the decision becomes a **multiple-attribute decision** and must be treated as such. Dedicated multiple-attribute decision-making techniques exist precisely to factor non-monetary criteria into the final choice.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt a29c4eeacc2005294c93] … [SWEBOK excerpt b4a03fe3cebc8941c34c]."*)

---

### 2. Define and balance your selection criteria carefully — neither too many nor too few

Work out explicitly what matters: license cost, performance, maturity, community activity, team familiarity, etc. Express each criterion as objectively as possible. Criteria that cannot be quantified in money are called **"unquantifiables," "irreducibles," or "intangibles"** — this is normal and expected. Balance is key: too many criteria make the analysis unwieldy, while too few may fail to distinguish between the three libraries.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt ad779e7ae7316b9713b2]."*)

---

### 3. Scale the number of criteria to the stakes of the decision

The more serious the consequences of picking the wrong library, the more selection criteria you should include. This justifies the effort of examining all five dimensions you listed (cost, performance, maturity, community, team familiarity) rather than only the licence fee.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 75413cf7ab971f0fb14a]."*)

---

### 4. Evaluate all alternatives on the same basis and over the same time horizon

When comparing costs, use a consistent financial metric (present worth, annual equivalent, etc.) and the same planning horizon for all three libraries. Applying a longer cost window to one candidate and a shorter one to another will make the shorter one look artificially cheaper.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 52782f508d83e0a6b786] … [SWEBOK excerpt 8c6be2eafa754bc08485]."*)

---

### 5. Use Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), not just the licence fee

The licence cost you see upfront is only part of the picture. TCO covers **all costs for acquiring, activating, and keeping the library running** — including training (especially for the library the team doesn't know), integration, maintenance, and support. These "not-so-obvious" costs can reverse the apparent ranking.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 9a3a3c18865d796f0f4c]."*)

---

### 6. Explicitly account for intangible assets — especially team knowledge

Team familiarity with a library is an **intangible asset**: employees' knowledge about tools and processes (tacit/implicit [SWEBOK excerpt 19088492ec788df0e679]." If they are not explicitly considered, you risk a choice that does not fit the organisation's actual capabilities.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt ae0618ae0b4b0b533dc7]) … [SWEBOK excerpt 5eed27bb7bc8930d99e2]."*)

---

### 7. Apply compensatory or non-compensatory multi-attribute techniques to combine the scores

You have two families of technique:

- **Compensatory** (e.g., additive weighting, AHP, Gilb's Impact Estimation, ATAM): all criteria are collapsed into a single figure of merit; a low score on one criterion can be offset by high scores on others.
- **Non-compensatory** (e.g., dominance, satisficing, lexicography): [SWEBOK excerpt d322441c7dc6d89d5f76] and trade-offs are not permitted.

Choose the family that matches your policy (e.g., use non-compensatory if, say, a minimum performance threshold is a hard requirement).

> (source: *"Compensatory techniques … [SWEBOK excerpt 6ed292052877d4475a99] … Non-compensatory techniques … [SWEBOK excerpt 6dc3a208e64ee94edbd5]."*)

---

### 8. Be objective and impartial when assigning weights; disclose any conflict of interest

Assigning relative weights to criteria (e.g., deciding that performance counts twice as much as community activity) is itself a value judgement. SWEBOK explicitly requires that this step be done with objectivity and impartiality, and that any conflict of interest (e.g., someone on the team who is a contributor to one library) be disclosed upfront.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 00f7fbea150e1817bf17]."*)

---

### 9. Perform a sensitivity analysis to test whether uncertainty can flip the decision

Your ratings for maturity, community activity, and performance will all be estimates, and estimates can be wrong. A **sensitivity analysis** checks whether plausible variations in those estimates would change the winner. If the same library wins across a wide range of assumptions, confidence in the choice is high; if the result is fragile, further investigation is warranted before committing.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 820c1d2bbc4d8e1e6c9d] … [SWEBOK excerpt 89fe88682dd0f65a7b5f]."* And: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 4b2f6ebfdcf3c83c2b23]."*)

---

### 10. Record the decision and its rationale formally

A library choice has long-lasting consequences (integration, maintenance, possible switching costs). SWEBOK advises that **high-consequence decisions should be formally documented**, including [SWEBOK excerpt 0c608c780d2dc1123418] it was chosen. This makes the decision defensible to stakeholders and auditable if the choice is ever revisited.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 9a58be7e9faa975f6362]."*)

---

### 11. Plan to verify the estimates after the decision is implemented

The quality of a multi-attribute [SWEBOK excerpt f00b0852de807c7ad183] feeding it. Close the loop by monitoring the chosen library's actual performance, maintenance costs, and team productivity against the estimates made during selection. This "closing of the loop" also improves future decisions.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 0a22155f16372db877ef] … [SWEBOK excerpt 758dd642afd15532fbb3]."*)

---

### 12. Consider the tool-selection questions specific to software components

When evaluating each library, SWEBOK suggests asking: What motivates acquisition from an organisational perspective? What constraints does the environment impose? Who will pay for training and maintenance? How adaptable is the tool? What is the plan for its future use? These questions surface hidden factors that pure licence-cost comparison misses.

> (source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt d49c26d77e05a8499e36] [[SWEBOK excerpt 86b32be877a0992950eb]? … [SWEBOK excerpt 22b97856baf9f8ca5f96]? … [SWEBOK excerpt e1d261bd50e7a1d74d89]? … [SWEBOK excerpt 0d8e7d5d9245201e6938]?"*)

---

**Summary of the process:** Define all criteria (monetary and non-monetary) → measure each library against each criterion consistently → weight the criteria honestly and impartially → aggregate scores using a compensatory or non-compensatory technique → stress-test the result with sensitivity analysis → document the rationale → monitor actual outcomes against estimates.
