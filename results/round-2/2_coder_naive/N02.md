## Guidelines for Evaluating a Proposed Networked-Services Architecture Before Implementation

The core problem—checking whether a high-level structure satisfies goals that pull against each other (response time, cost, modifiability)—sits squarely in the SWEBOK knowledge area on **Software Architecture Evaluation**. Here are the guidelines SWEBOK prescribes, each with its source.

---

### 1. First, Gather and Rank All Architecturally Significant Requirements (ASRs)

Before any evaluation can be meaningful, the team must surface every requirement that actually *shapes* the architecture—including the competing ones. [SWEBOK excerpt 7355675a3c5d6f7f4b7d]." [SWEBOK excerpt 5e5da7c24168149087a9]. When [SWEBOK excerpt 9f1a808e453d4d5f2421] simultaneously, **negotiation** is used to modify needs and expectations to make solutions possible.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt f219f5e403e144949b74]"* — SWEBOK Software Architecture KA, §3.2.1)

---

### 2. Explicitly Name Every Stakeholder and Their Competing Concerns

Response time, hosting cost, and ease of change each belong to different stakeholders. The list of architectural concerns SWEBOK enumerates includes "affordability, agility, availability … cost, deployability … evolvability … maintainability, modifiability … performance … scalability … usability." Each concern provides a separate basis for evaluation, so mapping each concern to a stakeholder role prevents one group's priorities from silently dominating the review.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 68f1cb133ce36eb3e742] … evolvability, extensibility … maintainability, modifiability, modularity … performance … scalability … testability, usability"* — SWEBOK Software Architecture KA, §1.2, Figure 2.2)

---

### 3. Apply the Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method (ATAM)

ATAM is SWEBOK's specifically named technique for exactly this problem. "The Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method (ATAM) [SWEBOK excerpt 02150566f2318db6d9d7]." Related methods—the [SWEBOK excerpt 418527e1cd3f462def29])—are also available. The team should run these *before* implementation, not after.

(source: *"The Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method (ATAM) [SWEBOK excerpt 02150566f2318db6d9d7]."* — SWEBOK Software Architecture KA, §4.1)

---

### 4. Build Multiple Architecture Views Targeted at Each Concern

A single diagram cannot reveal performance *and* cost *and* modifiability simultaneously. SWEBOK's viewpoint model addresses this: "[SWEBOK excerpt 8db6c1a9a1ae538920ac]." Relevant viewpoints include the **component-and-connector viewpoint** (runtime interactions and latency), the **deployment viewpoint** (hosting topology and cost), and the **module viewpoint** (implementation structure and future changeability). "[SWEBOK excerpt b6ee586f573f8cc59515] gives the right stakeholders a focused lens.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 8db6c1a9a1ae538920ac] … [SWEBOK excerpt 17b4d9b1daf8b8f138bf]."* — SWEBOK Software Architecture KA, §2.1)

---

### 5. Explicitly Address the Interactions *Between* Competing Concerns

SWEBOK warns that it is not enough to satisfy each concern in isolation: "[SWEBOK excerpt 04f5c8c3ca12dfd9aaa2] easy-to-[SWEBOK excerpt 4bdd60ab71ec1b86d925]." For networked services specifically, the team should trace how, say, fine-grained decomposition (good for modifiability) increases inter-service call overhead (bad for response time) and infrastructure count (bad for cost).

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 34199d3bc0e34a1edb24]."* — SWEBOK Software Architecture KA, §4.1)

---

### 6. Evaluate Explicitly Against Each Goodness Question

SWEBOK frames evaluation around concrete questions the team can pose: Is the architecture *[SWEBOK excerpt bf76e5350082d35de0be]*? Is it *[SWEBOK excerpt ad45cbd0c62aefc7ab25]* it? These map directly onto the team's three concerns: fitness for use ↔ response time; cost-effectiveness ↔ hosting cost; robustness over evolution ↔ ease of future change. Each question can be turned into a scored checklist item during the review.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 5412e694de87392412d9] [SWEBOK excerpt 7e51cdb9105c41500d8d]?"* — SWEBOK Software Architecture KA, §4.1)

---

### 7. Conduct a Structured Architecture Review (Preferably by a Third Party)

The evaluation should not be left to the proposing agent alone. "[SWEBOK excerpt 2c4b3a63bfe3df0388ac]." For the most rigorous result, consider *active reviews* (Parnas & Weiss's approach), [SWEBOK excerpt 3c5d69083e5dce234ec7]." Keeping the review independent of the proposer reduces confirmation bias.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 2c4b3a63bfe3df0388ac]."* — SWEBOK Software Architecture KA, §4.3)

---

### 8. Measure the Architecture with Structural Metrics

Qualitative review alone can miss quantifiable warning signs. SWEBOK identifies concrete metrics: "[SWEBOK excerpt bb913a49b727ee06e44f]." For the proposed service split, measuring inter-service coupling and component dependency reveals whether "ease of future change" is real or nominal—high coupling across service boundaries defeats the modularity goal.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 95ae979a26d714568a3f]."* — SWEBOK Software Architecture KA, §4.4)

---

### 9. Validate Behavior and Coverage with Use-Case Scenarios

Structural analysis needs to be paired with behavioral tracing. "[SWEBOK excerpt 47250df3263dc7898ea9] … [SWEBOK excerpt 5ee3de222c48ddecd1aa]." For networked services, trace the critical end-to-end response-time scenarios step-by-step across the proposed service boundaries. This converts an abstract latency concern into a concrete, verifiable sequence.

([SWEBOK excerpt a54bd3a19ad4ae34544e] … [SWEBOK excerpt 5ee3de222c48ddecd1aa]."* — SWEBOK Software Architecture KA, §4.2)

---

### 10. Apply Multiple-Attribute Decision-Making to Quantify the Trade-offs

When response time, hosting cost, and modifiability cannot all be maximized at once, the team needs a structured method to compare them. SWEBOK's Engineering Economics KA prescribes **multiple-attribute decision-making (MADM)**. Compensatory techniques (nondimensional scaling, additive weighting, Analytic Hierarchy Process) "[SWEBOK excerpt 6ed292052877d4475a99]" so that "[SWEBOK excerpt 250a28fea4ee20a0db0e] … a higher score in other criteria." ATAM itself is listed as a compensatory MADM technique. Critically, "[SWEBOK excerpt 145e734469479b7f70a7] [SWEBOK excerpt 70b849353f396ebaa2db]"—the team must agree on weights *before* scoring alternatives.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 3a11c63166fee03d5486] … [SWEBOK excerpt 785d16fb07ba30b9a8cf]."* — SWEBOK Engineering Foundations KA; and *"Gilb's Impact Estimation [SWEBOK excerpt d62e5ade89c458383496]) [SWEBOK excerpt d689423a7aa2ffb6ad23]."* — SWEBOK Software Engineering Economics KA, §6.1)

---

### 11. Use a Risk-Driven Approach to Prioritize Which Concerns to Analyse First

Not all three concerns carry equal risk in every context. SWEBOK endorses the risk-driven view: "[SWEBOK excerpt ca3b9581235f7fa04328]." If response-time requirements are extreme (e.g., SLA < 100 ms across services), analyse that concern first; if the team expects very frequent architectural change, modifiability risk is the priority.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt ca3b9581235f7fa04328]."* — SWEBOK Software Architecture KA, Further Readings: Fairbanks §16)

---

### 12. Build Targeted Prototypes or Simulations to Probe High-Risk Assumptions

Where analysis alone is inconclusive, SWEBOK endorses prototyping as a validation tool. "[SWEBOK excerpt af6795c3b205eeb2e19a] … [SWEBOK excerpt a0b00cf8f8a4a4ad0458]." Prototyping targets can include "an architectural design element or component." The team can build a minimal walking skeleton of two or three networked services, benchmark round-trip latency and resource consumption, and use that data to anchor the cost and response-time estimates before committing to the full design.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt af6795c3b205eeb2e19a] … [SWEBOK excerpt a0b00cf8f8a4a4ad0458]."* — SWEBOK Engineering Foundations KA, §6.3; and *"[SWEBOK excerpt 7dcfa7155d5ad128eb43] … [SWEBOK excerpt 1f5d53e661780c53c7b3]."* — SWEBOK Software Engineering Models and Methods KA, §4.4)
