Beyond code coverage, how can I evaluate whether my test suite is good at detecting faults?

### Answer

Here is a list of guidelines, drawn entirely from SWEBOK v4, for evaluating whether your test suite is good at detecting faults — beyond code coverage:

---

### 1. Use Mutation Testing (Mutation Score)
Compute the **mutation score**: the ratio of "killed" mutants to total generated mutants. [SWEBOK excerpt a47d64ce263982564d4e] software under test (SUT); a test case "kills" it when it detects the difference from the original. The higher the score, the better the suite's fault-detection ability.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt ae9ac78753f053c55ffe]."

---

### 2. Use Fault Injection
Artificially introduce known faults into the SUT before running your test suite, then observe how many are revealed. This provides a direct, empirical estimate of your suite's effectiveness and even allows an estimate of remaining genuine faults.

Track **fault density** — the ratio of discovered faults to SUT size. Also consider richer metrics like **fault depth** (the minimum number of fault removals needed to make the [SWEBOK excerpt cc3c948fabe72610fe33]).

### 6. Know the Fault Types You Are Targeting
Analyze what types of faults are likely to exist in the SUT and whether your test suite addresses them. Use fault taxonomies (e.g., orthogonal defect classification) to verify the suite's coverage of fault categories.
