### WHAT THE WRITE-UP MUST STATE

**1. State the purpose and intended outcomes of the workflow.**
A conforming process description must capture why the workflow exists and what it is meant to achieve, before specifying how to carry it out.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 537ed573346155dc567d] … [SWEBOK excerpt bd4aa8108f93732d6a85].")

---

**2. Name every activity and decompose each into concrete tasks.**
The description must break the workflow down to the level where individual required actions are unambiguous. A *process* is a set of cohesive activities; each activity is itself a set of tasks — required, recommended, or permissible actions intended to contribute to one or more outcomes.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt e5a2dca747487f12d7ff].'")

---

**3. Specify the inputs that enter each step and the outputs that leave it.**
Because the workflow passes work from person to person, every hand-off point must be defined in terms of what arrives and what must be produced. The output of one process becomes the input to the next.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 6ef52144d40031e30431].")

**4. Assign and document the actor (role) responsible for each action.**
Each step must identify who performs it. A well-known format for this is the actor-action structure: [SWEBOK excerpt 9eefa5538cfddb0d4757] the [SWEBOK excerpt 46b459e47543b6139ee2] may precede the actor, and the action may be followed by a condition or qualification.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 0a57bca5266a79c6ca80].")

**5. Document [SWEBOK excerpt 93326ab94475da9f1ec2].**
When the write-up is a plan-style document, it must cover all three of these elements so that readers — including new developers — can understand the full scope of the work.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 2904be6fea9fac60f3b9].")

**6. Keep the write-up as a "living document" and assign someone to maintain it.**
Processes evolve; a document that is not maintained becomes misleading. Configuration management plans, for example, are described as living documents updated throughout the life cycle, with a named person responsible for ensuring defined tasks are carried out correctly.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 18533f00d14448eef7cf] … [SWEBOK excerpt 9e6b8dc45c776239f263].")

**7. Treat the documented workflow as an organizational intangible asset — name it, value it, and protect it from loss.**
Your team's workflow is currently tacit knowledge held by two people. SWEBOK explicitly categorises documented business processes, checklists, procedures, and templates as intangible (knowledge) assets that give the organisation business value and need to be identified and preserved.

**8. Explicitly put a handoff process in place between roles, since its absence causes friction and knowledge loss.**
SWEBOK warns that when two groups exchange work without a written handoff process, friction and knowledge loss result. Capturing the handoff in writing mitigates this directly.

### IN WHAT FORMS CAN THE WRITE-UP BE WRITTEN?

**9. Use plain (unstructured) natural language for a simple, narrative description.**
Free-form prose such as "the system shall …" or a step-by-step narrative is a valid starting point and is recognised as a legitimate specification form, though it has limits in precision.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt f262d8176e894812ea3d] … [SWEBOK excerpt b455577cb937d3c42bbc].")

---

**10. Use structured natural language (e.g., actor-action templates, use-case tables) to increase precision and conciseness.**
Structured natural language imposes a consistent template on each step (trigger, actor, action, condition), reducing the ambiguity that plain prose introduces — especially important when the audience includes both humans and automated agents.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 4eecb83f896611f6153d].")

---

**11. Use graphical behavioural notations — flowcharts, activity diagrams, sequence diagrams, data-flow diagrams, or decision tables — to represent the dynamic flow.**
These complementary visual forms each capture a different aspect of the workflow: sequential control, concurrent paths, inter-role interactions, information flow, and branching logic.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 279f3097d19632185eb8] … [SWEBOK excerpt 72e682fb2d978f99f073] … [SWEBOK excerpt e81525b759a571e61ce3] … [SWEBOK excerpt 565e3e3abea2800a4b80] … [SWEBOK excerpt 8c2fbd91eb8bdf0c1813].")

**12. Use scripting or automation code as an executable form of the write-up for agents.**
When automated coding agents must follow the workflow, translating the written steps into scripts is itself a recognised form of documentation. Automating repetitive tasks reduces delays, increases quality, and ensures a consistent, stable operational environment — which is exactly what you want from your recurring workflow.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt eb4cda8d58a13633f62e] … [SWEBOK excerpt e7ed24e5aaafafeb4f29].")

---

**13. Store the write-up using any medium suitable for the purpose — wikis, documents, and more — and document the operations steps and tools.**
SWEBOK does not mandate a single medium; what matters is that the documentation is accessible and covers the steps and the tools they rely on.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 9dcb01478b5182dee8a0]).")

---

**14. Consider formal or model-based notation if ambiguity must be minimised for automated processing.**
More formal models have [SWEBOK excerpt 80d57d4729dfd35e966d] analysed, making them especially suited to driving automated agents without misinterpretation.

> (source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 8929d16b5fb5ae09f349] [mechanically analysed [SWEBOK excerpt b99fcfd568b556f76339]].")

### Summary

In short, SWEBOK tells you that your write-up **must** cover purpose, outcomes, activities, tasks, inputs, outputs, roles, and hand-off points. It **can** take the form of plain prose, structured templates, graphical diagrams (flowcharts, activity/sequence/data-flow diagrams, decision tables), executable scripts, or formal/model-based notation — and ideally uses several of these forms together, stored in any accessible medium, and treated as a living organisational asset with a named maintainer.
