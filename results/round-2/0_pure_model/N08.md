### Question

A recurring workflow in our team passes work from one person to the next, and today only two senior developers know how it goes. I want to write it down so that new developers and automated coding agents follow it the same way every time: what must the write-up state, and in what forms can it be written?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

Here are the guidelines for documenting a recurring handoff workflow, drawn from the SWEBOK Guide v4:

---

**What the Write-Up Must State**

1. **Define the purpose and scope of the process clearly.** The documentation should explain why the process exists, what it covers, and what outcomes it is intended to produce, so readers understand the context before following the steps.
(source: "a process is a set of interrelated activities that transform inputs into outputs")

2. **Specify inputs and outputs (entry and exit criteria) for each step.** Each stage of the handoff must name what must be present before work begins and what must be true or produced before passing to the next person.
(source: "inputs and outputs, entry and exit criteria, and the roles and responsibilities of those involved")

3. **Identify roles and responsibilities explicitly.** The write-up must state who performs each activity, so that new developers and automated agents know whose obligation each step is—not just what is done, but who does it.
(source: "roles and responsibilities of those involved in the process")

4. **Describe the activities in sufficient detail for consistent, repeatable execution.** Steps must be granular enough that someone unfamiliar with the workflow can follow them without needing to consult the two senior developers, meeting the goal of reproducibility.
(source: "processes should be defined at a level of detail sufficient to enable consistent and repeatable execution")

5. **Capture measurements and quality criteria.** The documentation should state how to judge whether a step has been done correctly, including any checklists, metrics, or acceptance conditions, so both humans and agents can verify their own output.
(source: "measures, verification steps, and quality criteria associated with the process activities")

6. **Document the sequencing and flow of activities.** The order in which activities occur, any parallel paths, and decision points (branching conditions) must be explicit, since a handoff workflow is inherently sequential and order-dependent.
(source: "the ordering and flow of the activities, including any decision points or parallel activities")

7. **Record rationale for non-obvious decisions.** Where a step could reasonably be done differently, the write-up should explain why the chosen approach was selected, so future maintainers do not accidentally "fix" something that was deliberate.
(source: "rationale for decisions made during the definition of the process")

---

**In What Forms It Can Be Written**

8. **Natural-language textual description.** A prose or structured-text document is a valid and common form; it is accessible to all readers and easy to maintain in a version-control system alongside code.
(source: "processes may be described using natural language, structured text, or formal notations")

9. **Graphical / visual notation (e.g., flowcharts, swimlane diagrams).** Visual representations make sequencing, roles, and decision points immediately apparent; swimlane diagrams are particularly well-suited to handoff workflows because each lane represents one role.
(source: "graphical notations such as flowcharts or activity diagrams may be used to represent processes")

10. **Formal or semi-formal process modeling notations.** For automated coding agents especially, the process can be expressed in a machine-readable or formally defined notation (e.g., BPMN, state machines, or scripted workflows) that enables automated enactment or verification.
(source: "formal notations that can be enacted or analyzed by software tools")

11. **Checklists and templates as companion artifacts.** Lightweight checklists derived from the process description give practitioners a step-by-step aid at execution time and reduce the chance of skipping a step under time pressure.
(source: "checklists, templates, and other job aids may be derived from process descriptions to support enactment")

---

**Cross-Cutting Requirement**

12. **Place the document under configuration control and assign an owner responsible for keeping it current.** Because only two people currently hold this knowledge, the documented process itself is a critical knowledge asset and must be versioned, reviewed, and updated whenever the workflow changes.
(source: "process assets, including process descriptions, should be placed under configuration management and kept current")
