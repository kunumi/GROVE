- Recognize that the decision is a multiple-attribute problem: financial cost (license fees) is usually the most important criterion, but it is not always the only one. - Define your selection criteria explicitly before evaluating candidates. Each criterion should be expressed as objectively as possible — ideally in monetary terms, but criteria that cannot be monetised (called 'unquantifiables', 'irreducibles', or 'intangibles') are still valid selection criteria. Aim for a balanced number: too many criteria make the analysis unwieldy, while too few may not differentiate well between proposals and could lead to a suboptimal choice. (source: root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Evaluating Candidates and Managing Decision Costs/Solution Generation and Feasibility Verification.md) [JEV 0.95]
- When comparing the financial dimension (license cost) across the three libraries, use the same basis for comparison — the same planning horizon and the same categories of cost and income — so that no alternative is artificially favoured by a shorter or longer time frame. (source: root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Establishing Criteria and Evaluation Methods/Evaluating Alternatives Against Selection Criteria.md) [JEV 0.97]
- Choose a compensatory technique — such as nondimensional scaling, additive weighting, or the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) — to collapse all criteria (monetary and non-monetary) into a single figure of merit. With compensatory techniques, a lower score on one criterion (e.g., higher license cost) can be offset by a higher score on others (e.g., better performance or greater team familiarity), making the trade-offs explicit and transparent. (source: root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory Techniques for Multiple Criteria Assessment.md; root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory and Non-Compensatory Decision-Making Techniques.md) [JEV 0.95]
- Alternatively, apply a non-compensatory technique (dominance, satisficing, or lexicography) if certain criteria are absolute constraints rather than trade-offs. For example, if a library fails a minimum performance threshold it is eliminated regardless of cost savings, because non-compensatory techniques treat each criterion as a separate entity and do not allow trade-offs among criteria. (source: root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory and Non-Compensatory Decision-Making Techniques.md) [JEV 0.91]
- Treat team knowledge ('know-how') as an intangible asset that must be explicitly considered in the decision. Intangible assets — including employees' knowledge about processes and tools (tacit knowledge) and institutional knowledge (explicit knowledge) — are usually hidden but can significantly affect financial performance. [SWEBOK excerpt a068a951c4049fb56c26] important it is to surface and weigh these hidden factors. (source: root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Identifying and Characterizing Intangible Assets.md; root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Evaluating Complex Proposals and Strategic Alternatives/Do-Nothing Alternative and Intangible Assets in Decision-Making.md) [JEV 0.96]
- Characterise each library's intangible dimensions (maturity, community activity, team familiarity) [SWEBOK excerpt 589448feddf01a1a7f43] organisational assets. Assign thresholds for 'acceptable' and 'unacceptable' levels of impact and quality, so that a library that falls below a threshold in a critical dimension can be clearly flagged — even if its financial score is attractive. (source: root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Characterizing Intangible Assets by Impact and Quality.md) [JEV 0.91]
- Always keep the 'do-nothing' alternative in view: if none of the three libraries scores acceptably across all criteria, the defensible choice may be to continue with the current approach or seek a fourth option rather than force-selecting an unsatisfactory candidate. (source: root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Evaluating Complex Proposals and Strategic Alternatives/Do-Nothing Alternative and Intangible Assets in Decision-Making.md) [JEV 0.88]
