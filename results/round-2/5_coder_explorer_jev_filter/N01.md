- Ensure each requirement statement is unambiguous (interpretable in only one way), testable/quantified so compliance can be clearly demonstrated, atomic (representing a single decision), and expressed in stakeholder vocabulary. Apply the 5-whys technique to converge on the true problem rather than accepting a stated solution. (source: root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md) [JEV 0.96]
- Replace loose prose with structured natural language requirements specifications, which impose constraints on how requirements are expressed (e.g., actor-action format) to increase precision and conciseness and reduce misinterpretation. - Use Acceptance Test-Driven Development (ATDD) or Behaviour-Driven Development (BDD): before any design or construction work begins, bring together one or more software engineers, business domain experts, and QA/test professionals to agree on a set of acceptance test cases that must pass to demonstrate that the feature has been correctly implemented. (source: root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Engineering_ Viability, Verification, and Stakeholder Management/Formal Analysis in Requirements Engineering.md; root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Behavioral Models and Requirements Documentation/Behavioral Modeling Approaches and Formality Levels/Formal Modeling Methods for Requirements Specification.md) [JEV 0.96]
- Document explicit acceptance criteria and additional attributes (rationale, source stakeholder, dependencies, conflicts) alongside each requirement statement to help the whole team interpret and manage requirements consistently. - Conduct multi-perspective requirements reviews: have clients/customers/users confirm that their needs are completely and accurately represented; have requirements-specialist engineers check clarity and conformance to standards; and have design/construction engineers verify [SWEBOK excerpt 28ec65f9a9aa744ceca0]. - Use simulated or executed specifications to validate requirements with non-technical stakeholders: hand-interpret or execute the formal/model-based specification across a sufficient set of demonstration scenarios to convince stakeholders [SWEBOK excerpt 94ed6d0c150df051652f]. (source: root/Requirements Validation and Management/Requirements Validation Methods and Techniques.md) [JEV 0.96]
- Build a prototype to validate requirements when the specification cannot be directly simulated: prototypes expose engineers' assumptions and give stakeholders and domain experts concrete feedback on whether the team's interpretation of the feature is correct. (source: root/Requirements Validation and Management/Prototyping for Requirements Validation and Feedback.md) [JEV 0.96]
- Use facilitated elicitation workshops (JAD, JRP) and structured techniques such as user story mapping, usage scenario descriptions, and task analysis to collaboratively surface tacit knowledge from domain experts that is unlikely to surface from prose alone. (source: root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Stakeholder Identification and Requirements Elicitation Techniques/Stakeholder Elicitation Techniques and Requirements Sources.md) [JEV 0.87]
- When the product owner and engineers read the same prose differently, treat this as a requirements conflict and negotiate a consensus resolution among all conflicting stakeholders. It is unwise for the engineer to make a unilateral decision; resolution should be traceable back to the customer.
