## Guidelines for Tracing a Recurring Failure Back to Its True Root Cause

---

### 1. Apply formal Root Cause Analysis (RCA) rather than patching symptoms

Treat every recurrence as evidence that only the symptom was fixed. Use RCA methods whose explicit purpose is to explain *why* and *how* an undesirable outcome happened, so the organization can take effective action to prevent recurrence — not just contain the latest instance.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 738aea7097743e1c099a]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Standards, Quality Assurance, and Modern Manufacturing Systems/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Application/Engineering Standards and Root Cause Analysis Methods.md)

---

### 2. Use the 5-Whys technique to drill past the first plausible cause

Start from the observed failure and repeat the question "Why?" in successive cycles until the answer would leave the stakeholder's core problem unsolved if left unaddressed. The technique is explicitly named "5-whys" to push engineers to continue well past the first obvious explanation.

(source: "The 5-whys technique … [SWEBOK excerpt e524a6814dc318ae160a]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Standards, Quality Assurance, and Modern Manufacturing Systems/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Application/Root Cause Analysis Techniques.md; and: "[SWEBOK excerpt eeada791e08cb7ee0327] … [SWEBOK excerpt 5f3667199e384ca74216]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md)

---

### 3. Build a cause-and-effect (Ishikawa / fishbone) diagram to surface all contributing categories

Decompose the failure into successive levels of potential causes — people, processes, tools, materials, measurements, and environment — using an Ishikawa diagram. This visual structure prevents the team from anchoring on one plausible cause while overlooking others.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 74539f2164ca00a5c989] [SWEBOK excerpt 9b0dc347de3e5f46ef18]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Standards, Quality Assurance, and Modern Manufacturing Systems/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Application/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Techniques.md)

---

### 4. Apply Fault Tree Analysis (FTA) to distinguish "or" from "and" causal relationships

When several factors may each independently trigger the failure, or when only a specific *combination* of factors causes it, use FTA. Unlike basic fishbone charts, FTA explicitly models whether causes combine conjunctively (all required) or disjunctively (any one sufficient), producing a more accurate causal model.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 15cdd1f2cb3eea077cd1]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Standards, Quality Assurance, and Modern Manufacturing Systems/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Application/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Techniques.md)

---

### 5. Use change analysis to find what differed between failing and non-failing situations

Compare contexts where the failure occurred with structurally similar situations where it did not. The root cause is likely located in whatever differs between the two, not in the factors that are identical.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 393788cc60b3b2c6fee3]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Standards, Quality Assurance, and Modern Manufacturing Systems/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Application/Root Cause Analysis Techniques.md)

---

### 6. Establish and use a defect classification taxonomy before collecting data

Numbers alone — counts of how many times the failure was patched — are insufficient to identify underlying causes. Engineers must first establish a meaningful defect classification scheme so that semantic information is attached to each occurrence and patterns become visible.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt cc445036fb1c8bc4d733]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/Quality Metrics, Reliability Models, and Corrective Actions/Corrective and Preventive Actions in Quality Management.md; and: "[SWEBOK excerpt bcc82cdbad3e6dcf9632] … [SWEBOK excerpt d6e4dea0d8f5480e7529]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/Software Quality Management and Defect Classification.md)

---

### 7. Apply Pareto analysis and frequency-severity prioritisation to select the right problem to solve first

Before investing in deep RCA, rank undesirable outcomes by how frequently they occur and how much resource they consume. This ensures the investigation focuses on the highest-impact recurring failure, not just the most recent one.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 75158273419b971598cf]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Standards, Quality Assurance, and Modern Manufacturing Systems/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Application/Root Cause Analysis in Process Improvement.md)

---

### 8. Gather structured evidence — testimony, historical trends, experiments — before naming a cause

Avoid premature hypotheses. Systematically collect statements, [SWEBOK excerpt a8c04145b18e19cefdda] that surround the failure before identifying its cause, so the conclusion is evidence-based rather than intuitive.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 1b9b9c1bb4af147d9369]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Standards, Quality Assurance, and Modern Manufacturing Systems/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Application/Root Cause Analysis in Process Improvement.md)

### 9. Conduct post-mortems and assemble a multidisciplinary team for recurring production problems

Recurring production problems may have an underlying cause in infrastructure or system components that no single specialist can see. Convene a multidisciplinary team and perform proper post-mortems after every incident to find the source and implement solutions that prevent similar incidents from happening again.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 9b89c65485c19d20fd67]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Engineering Operations Control Techniques.md; and: "[SWEBOK excerpt baea92134ba15f083890]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Rollback and Problem Resolution in DevOps.md)

### 10. Use program slicing to isolate precisely which statements can affect the failing variable

For code-level investigations, compute the program slice — the set of statements that might affect the value of the variable at the point of failure. Program slicing can be performed statically or dynamically and directly narrows the search space for the error source.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 62d27e75e2c5066c084e]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Development Systems and Quality Assurance/Software Construction Techniques and Tools/Code Profiling and Program Slicing Techniques.md)

### 11. Use dynamic analysers, data flow analysers, and dependency analysers to trace execution and data paths

Tracing tools record execution history; data flow analysers track all possible data flows; dependency analysers reveal interrelationships among components. Together they let maintainers follow the actual runtime path that led to the failure, rather than reasoning from source code alone.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt d4955ce0a3cf22030707] … [SWEBOK excerpt aa269267f3e18256a483]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Maintenance and Configuration Management/Software Maintenance, Evolution, and Quality Assurance/Continuous Integration, Delivery, and Software Maintenance/Software Maintenance Tools and Technologies.md)

---

### 12. Isolate, identify, and describe the fault before attempting a fix

Do not remove a defect until a debugging effort has explicitly isolated it, identified it, and described it. Skipping this step is precisely what produces patches that resolve the symptom while leaving the underlying fault intact.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 044483db3c94f12dc72c]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Test Generation, Environment Setup, and Incident Management/Test Incident Reporting and Documentation Process.md)

### 13. Select corrective actions that eliminate (or disconnect) the cause, not just the symptom

Once the root cause is identified, choose corrective actions [SWEBOK excerpt 418489a77daae6c5d56b] organisation's control, (3) meet organisational goals, and (4) do not introduce new problems. Candidate actions should eliminate the cause, reduce its probability, or disconnect it from the effect.

### 14. Observe corrective actions after implementation to confirm they are working

RCA is not complete when the fix is deployed. Monitor and observe the selected corrective actions in production to verify efficiency and effectiveness. Without this step there is no feedback confirming whether the true root cause was actually addressed.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 3b338484aea1dce0cd50]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making Through Data and Standards/Standards, Quality Assurance, and Modern Manufacturing Systems/Root Cause Analysis Methods and Application/Root Cause Analysis in Process Improvement.md)

### 15. Use reliability models and defect profiles to anticipate where the failure class is likely to recur

Build reliability models from failure data already collected in production. Use this information [SWEBOK excerpt 6e8801e12a5429130552], and to develop defect profiles that guide where to focus quality effort on future work in the same domain.

(source: "[SWEBOK excerpt 1222b5faffa6381b1861] … [SWEBOK excerpt 153e2e04dd0ec4b37a72] … [SWEBOK excerpt 5589f6bd6ce4725875e1]." — /workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/Software Quality Assurance and Verification Standards/Software Quality Assurance and Defect Management/Quality Metrics, Reliability Models, and Corrective Actions/Software Quality Measurements and Reliability Models.md)

### 16. Embed corrective and preventive actions in the Quality Management System and track them independently

Document corrective actions and submit them to an organisation independent of the current project so they can be monitored and stakeholders kept informed. This prevents fixes from being quietly closed without verification that the problem has stopped recurring.
