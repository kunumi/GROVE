## Guidelines for Evaluating Test Suite Fault-Detection Quality Beyond Code Coverage

---

### 1. Use fault injection (error seeding) and estimate the remaining genuine faults

Artificially inject known faults into the SUT, run the test suite, and observe how many are detected. The number of artificial faults found gives a proxy for the suite's ability to find real ones, and allows estimating the number of genuine faults still present.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 243d9c57faf056f32a15]."*

⚠️ Apply this with care: the SWEBOK notes that statisticians question whether injected faults are representative of real ones, and that there is a risk of leaving seeded faults in the SUT.

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation.md`)

---

### 2. Compute and maximise the mutation score

Run mutation testing: generate many slightly modified versions of the SUT (mutants), execute the test suite against each, and calculate the **mutation score** = killed mutants ÷ total generated mutants. The higher the score, the stronger the suite's fault-detection ability.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt c55903435d9db2cf30b2]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation Testing and Fault Injection Evaluation.md`)

---

### 3. Understand and exploit the coupling-effect assumption of mutation testing

When using mutation testing, rely on the fact that detecting simple, syntactic faults in mutants correlates with the ability to detect more complex, real faults — the *coupling effect*. For the technique to be meaningful, mutants must be generated and executed systematically and in large numbers.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 21d2d5391343f6cd3372] [SWEBOK excerpt 92398cd5ba1475bae1f8]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Fault Models and Mutation Testing Techniques.md`)

---

### 4. Treat mutation testing as a test-adequacy criterion in its own right

Do not only use mutation testing retrospectively. Use it prospectively: generate test cases until enough mutants are killed, or deliberately design test cases to kill surviving mutants. This turns the mutation score into a stopping criterion for test-suite development.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 27a4bec9870baa6ea4ec]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Mutation-Based and Usage-Based Testing Techniques/Mutation Testing as a Testing Criterion.md`)

---

### 5. Measure fault density to evaluate the SUT and inform test sufficiency

Track **fault density** — the ratio of discovered faults to SUT size. Consider supplementary metrics such as *fault depth* (the minimum number of fault removals needed to make the [SWEBOK excerpt c25c39f029a07db7b679]) to get a richer picture.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt fcd099864e112f4cbf75] … [SWEBOK excerpt b3a64b24f27702cd980d] … and fault multiplicity."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Fault Density Measurement in Software Testing.md`)

---

### 6. Compare test techniques by their relative effectiveness on fault-related properties

Do not rely on a single metric. Compare techniques against several properties: how many tests are [SWEBOK excerpt fc34bb92c0fdd213fb95] faults found through testing to all [SWEBOK excerpt 89f26d2f4c5a1747fa0b].

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt c4ad890b5668c6dfc1ec]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Test Effectiveness and Process Levels/Test Suite Effectiveness and Testing Process Integration.md`)

---

### 7. Treat coverage and fault-density estimates as *necessary but not sufficient* stopping criteria

Acknowledge that thoroughness measures such as code/functional coverage and fault density estimates provide useful support for deciding when to stop, but they are not sufficient on their own. Balance them against cost and risk considerations.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 268c49677beedce8c7ad]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/Test Completion and Knowledge Management/Test Completion and Artifact Reusability in Software Testing.md`)

---

### 8. Apply fault-based testing techniques that target likely or predefined fault categories

Design test cases specifically to reveal known categories of faults (using fault models such as Orthogonal Defect Classification). This focuses the test suite on weaknesses most likely to harbour real defects and makes the suite more discriminating than one designed only for coverage.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 860d2453eaf6e85c9e45]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Testing Approaches and Execution Strategies/Smoke Testing and Knowledge-Based Testing Approaches.md`)

---

### 9. Use error guessing grounded in fault history and expertise

Complement systematic techniques with **error guessing**: have experienced engineers design test cases targeting the most plausible faults, drawing on the history of faults found in earlier projects. This taps knowledge that structural or specification-based techniques may miss.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 58750920721c36886ebb]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Testing Approaches and Execution Strategies/Software Testing Techniques and Methodologies/Experience-Based Techniques for Test Suite Generation.md`)

---

### 10. Define and select proper test-effectiveness measures aligned to the test objective

Match the measure to what you are actually trying to evaluate. Measurement is fundamental to quality analysis; without well-defined effectiveness measures it is impossible to judge whether a test objective has been reached or whether testing can be stopped.

> *"[SWEBOK excerpt 129754ae2db3d73df81f]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing Levels, Objectives, and Approaches/Scalability, Test Effectiveness, and Testing Controllability.md`)

---

### 11. Use injection-based tools to systematically reproduce and confirm fault scenarios

Complement analysis with **injection-based tools** that introduce or reproduce specific problems, confirming that the SUT (and therefore the test suite) behaves appropriately under those conditions.

> *"Injection-based tools … [SWEBOK excerpt 72e21d697b157ba3190e]."*

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Testing, Operations, and Maintenance/Software Operations and Maintenance Practices/Software Testing Tools and Standards/Software Testing Tools and Techniques/Software Testing and Reliability Evaluation Tools.md`)
