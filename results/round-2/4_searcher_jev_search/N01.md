## Guidelines for Unambiguously Pinning Down a Feature

### 1. Treat ambiguity as the core threat and name it explicitly
Acknowledge from the start that *"[SWEBOK excerpt b3d3baa4d56724d56b3d]."* Write the problem statement for the feature so everyone agrees on the actual problem before discussing any solution.

### 2. Identify and involve every relevant stakeholder class
Perform a stakeholder analysis to surface all important stakeholder classes before writing a single requirement. Missing voices bias requirements toward better-represented groups and introduce hidden ambiguities.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt f0b4c74eb9bf6d40ce93]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Stakeholder Identification and Requirements Elicitation Techniques/Stakeholders and Requirements Elicitation/Stakeholder Analysis for Balanced Requirements.md`)

---

### 3. Run facilitated workshops and collaborative elicitation sessions
Hold joint application development (JAD) sessions, brainstorming meetings, or similar facilitated workshops with domain experts to surface tacit requirements that stakeholders often leave unstated.

(source: *"Common stakeholder elicitation techniques include … [SWEBOK excerpt a56bd79a8b04da0f7d3f]), joint requirements planning (JRP) and other facilitated workshops … [SWEBOK excerpt db85e738fbf99d25f9af]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Stakeholder Identification and Requirements Elicitation Techniques/Stakeholder Elicitation Techniques and Requirements Sources.md`)

---

### 4. Apply the 5-Whys to reach the true problem before specifying anything
Ask "Why is this the requirement?" repeatedly until the team can answer "[SWEBOK excerpt a2acec7a985d97bfc05c]." This prevents specifying a solution instead of the actual need.

(source: *"The 5-whys technique … [SWEBOK excerpt 716db07833277b41892e].'"* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md`)

---

### 5. Make each requirement unambiguous, testable, atomic, and expressed in stakeholder vocabulary
Check every requirement statement against these properties: it must be *interpretable in only one way*, *quantified so compliance can be clearly demonstrated*, represent *a single decision*, and *use stakeholder vocabulary*.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 6eabf6d17bfa31257476]; … atomic, represent a single decision; … [SWEBOK excerpt 825d8b93845096610f26]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md`)

---

### 6. Replace prose with structured natural language (actor-action format)
Impose grammatical constraints on how requirements are stated. Use an actor-action structure — *"When [triggering event], [actor] shall [action] [unless/condition]"* — to remove syntactic ambiguity and make the responsible party and the expected behaviour explicit.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt c6277fe69265442c6926]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Natural Language Requirements Specifications/Structured Natural Language Requirements Specification.md`)

---

### 7. Agree on acceptance test cases (ATDD) before any design or construction begins
Bring software engineers, business domain experts, and QA professionals together — before any production code or design work — to agree on the exact test cases that must pass to prove the feature is correctly implemented.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt cd5cc3b984affe631643] [SWEBOK excerpt acf2737e4c17b2e47a7b]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Test-Driven Development and Acceptance Testing/Acceptance Criteria-Based Requirements Specification.md`)

---

### 8. Write scenarios in BDD's Given-When-Then format
Express each acceptance scenario as *"Given \<context\>, when \<stimulus\>, then \<outcome\>."* Because test-case language is precise rather than natural language, this format is preferred by domain experts and directly eliminates ambiguity.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 9ed61930fe91c23f779a]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Specification and Validation Techniques/Test-Driven Development and Requirements Specification/Acceptance Criteria and BDD Scenarios for Banking Systems.md`)

---

### 9. Cover all scenarios including boundary, exception, and negative cases
Write scenarios not only for the happy path but also for boundary conditions, exception conditions, and negative cases (e.g., disabled card, insufficient balance). The overall set of requirements must be complete in that it *"adequately addresses boundary conditions, exception conditions and security needs."*

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt b8010e9e221cc4cf004e]"* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Requirements Analysis and Refinement/Software Requirements Properties and Quality Criteria.md`)

---

### 10. Conduct multi-perspective requirements reviews before any code is written
Hold a formal review in which clients/users verify their needs are represented, requirements-specialist engineers check for clarity and conformance, and developers check that the specification is sufficient to support their work.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt dfae591af3c0604a8a36] … [SWEBOK excerpt 6ff151b00fdca3243a39] … [SWEBOK excerpt 3029ae1319171a4f6a92]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Requirements Validation and Management/Requirements Validation Methods and Techniques.md`)

---

### 11. Use checklists or a "definition of done" to focus reviewers
Provide reviewers with checklists or quality criteria so they [SWEBOK excerpt 9de05a754f81b2acffbb] practice — not just surface readability.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 8e950e70fb5af5c32b24]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Requirements Validation and Management/Requirements Validation Methods and Techniques.md`)

---

### 12. Build a prototype or executable simulation when text alone is insufficient
When a specification cannot be fully reviewed as text, build a prototype or run a simulation so that nontechnical stakeholders can observe the behaviour and confirm — or correct — the engineers' interpretation.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 78d5d553044949c2fea4] … [SWEBOK excerpt 786472a44a8cccda297d]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Requirements Validation and Management/Prototyping for Requirements Validation and Feedback.md`)

---

### 13. Consider model-based specification (UML/SysML) for the most ambiguity-prone parts
Where natural language remains prone to misreading even after structuring, use a modelling language (UML, SysML) instead. The more formal the model, the less ambiguous it is.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt ace220df203c699ad5ba]) … [SWEBOK excerpt 243ec5ba82cc25e0ed3b]."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Model-Based Requirements Specification Approaches.md`)

---

### 14. Record rationale, source, and acceptance criteria as supplemental requirement attributes
For each requirement, document *why* it exists (rationale), *who* imposed it (source), and its *acceptance criteria*. This context prevents reinterpretation over time and makes the intent traceable.

(source: *"[SWEBOK excerpt 4a58558b48bfd138fc04] … Possible additional attributes include: … [SWEBOK excerpt 59d7cf197b35b182a65d]); … acceptance criteria."* — `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Software Requirements_ Foundations and Engineering Practices/Requirements Engineering_ Elicitation, Analysis, and Specification/Behavioral Models and Requirements Documentation/Formal Requirements Models and Additional Attributes.md`)
