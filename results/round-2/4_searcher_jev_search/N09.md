## Guidelines for Combining Quantitative and Non-Quantitative Criteria to Choose Among Three Candidate Libraries

---

### 1. Recognise from the outset that this is a multiple-attribute decision, not a simple cost comparison

Money is usually the most important criterion, but it is not the only one. When other attributes matter, the decision must be treated explicitly as a multiple-attribute decision, and dedicated techniques must be used to handle the non-monetary dimensions.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt b8ffe784216ab5901a82]."

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Multiple-Attribute Decision-Making Beyond Financial Criteria.md`)

### 2. Explicitly name and accept the intangible criteria rather than ignoring them

Attributes like team familiarity, community activity, and library maturity cannot be expressed in monetary terms. The SWEBOK calls such criteria "unquantifiables," "irreducibles," or "intangibles." Trying to force every criterion into a dollar figure distorts the analysis; instead, acknowledge these attributes and treat them alongside financial ones.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt a4318de5004b25042ec1].'"

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multiple-Attribute Decision Criteria and Evaluation.md`)

---

### 3. Choose the right number of criteria — neither too few nor too many

Defining the criteria is a subjective task with real stakes in both directions. An overly short list may fail to distinguish between the three libraries, while an unwieldy list makes the analysis impractical.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt ad779e7ae7316b9713b2]."

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multiple-Attribute Decision Criteria and Evaluation.md`)

Additionally, adding more criteria must be weighed against the extra effort required:

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Establishing Criteria and Evaluation Methods/Balancing Decision Criteria Against Analysis Effort.md`)

---

### 4. Evaluate all alternatives against each criterion on the same footing

Each library candidate must be judged with the same basis of comparison. When cost is involved, use the same planning horizon for all three; do not compare one library's total cost of ownership over five years against another's one-year licence fee, as this will artificially favour the shorter view.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt 52782f508d83e0a6b786] … [SWEBOK excerpt 8c6be2eafa754bc08485]."

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Establishing Criteria and Evaluation Methods/Evaluating Alternatives Against Selection Criteria.md`)

---

### 5. Apply a compensatory technique to collapse all criteria into a single defensible figure of merit

Compensatory techniques — such as nondimensional scaling, additive weighting, or the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) — allow a lower score on one criterion (e.g., higher licence cost) to be traded off against a higher score on another (e.g., greater team familiarity). This is the natural fit when no single criterion is an absolute disqualifier.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt dd90612de75ae5a7ee4c])."

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory and Non-Compensatory Decision-Making Techniques.md`)

---

### 6. Consider non-compensatory techniques when certain criteria must not be traded away

If one or more criteria represent hard constraints (e.g., a library with an incompatible licence is simply excluded regardless of its performance score), use non-compensatory techniques instead. Dominance, satisficing, or lexicography treat each criterion as a separate filter, disallowing trade-offs.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt ceccdfe5823e12c32f0a]."

(source: `/workspace/swebok-v4/root/Engineering Decision-Making and Financial Analysis/Multi-Criteria Decision-Making and Intangible Assets/Compensatory and Non-Compensatory Decision-Making Techniques.md`)

### 7. Identify and account for the intangible assets your organisation brings to the decision

Team knowledge of a library is an intangible asset — knowledge residing within the organisation that affects outcomes. Such assets must be explicitly surfaced and considered; ignoring them risks choosing a solution that does not fit the organisation, no matter how good its standalone characteristics.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt 1b7568245a34d0519644]. … [SWEBOK excerpt 90b91ddf1b37bca41ebd]."

The final choice among the three libraries should be driven by multiple explicit criteria that together reflect the organisation's business objectives. There is no single rule; all relevant dimensions must be weighed together.

### 10. Address estimation uncertainty with ranges, sensitivity analysis, and delayed final decisions

Performance figures, future maintenance costs, and community longevity are estimates, not certainties. If the degree of inaccuracy could change the outcome, do not collapse immediately to a point estimate. Use ranges, perform sensitivity analysis to see which criteria drive the result, and avoid committing prematurely.

### 11. Consider the acquisition-specific risks: licence terms, security, and supply-chain exposure

Third-party libraries — including open-source ones — carry legal and security obligations. The licence must be reviewed for restrictions on use. Unfiltered access to external repositories creates attack surfaces. Acquired components must be verified for completeness, correctness, and fitness before integration.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt 184a2ab9d57169b59ee8]. … [SWEBOK excerpt e3ca00f82a82d0e84520]. … [SWEBOK excerpt 991ef6bbbff3030dc046]."

### 12. Record the rationale for the decision — including rejected alternatives and the reasons for rejecting them

A defensible choice is one whose logic can be reconstructed later. Document which alternatives were considered, which criteria and trade-offs drove the selection, and why each of the other two libraries was rejected. This prevents the team from inadvertently revisiting a rejected option when conditions have not changed, and it makes the decision auditable.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt 7c1b161d2dc2335116a7]."

### 13. Package the entire analysis in a business case to communicate the decision to stakeholders

The result of the evaluation should be consolidated into a business case that presents the recommendation from multiple perspectives — cost, benefit, risk, and intangibles — in a form that decision-makers and other stakeholders can scrutinise and challenge.

> "[SWEBOK excerpt 1ee15035950f0af3b9aa]."
