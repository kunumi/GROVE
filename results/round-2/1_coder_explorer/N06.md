### Question

At the end of each release our bug tracker tells us only how many bugs were found, and that count gives the team nothing to act on. What should we do with our bug records so that they help us introduce fewer bugs in later releases?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
SWEBOK v4 makes clear that a bare defect count is insufficient: teams must classify defects into a taxonomy, record them persistently, perform root-cause analysis to eliminate sources rather than just symptoms, compute richer metrics such as fault density, and feed everything back into the organization's measurement database and future test-planning activities. This transforms the bug tracker from a scoreboard into a process-improvement engine.

# Guidelines

- **Establish a defect classification taxonomy.** Raw counts are meaningless without categorisation. Create a taxonomy to describe and categorize anomalies found during reviews and testing.

- **Record every defect; don't let the data disappear.** "[SWEBOK excerpt 1bd4e9984ae7478574ad]."
  (source: same file as above)

- **Perform Root Cause Analysis (RCA) on the recorded defects.** Beyond fixing individual bugs, "[SWEBOK excerpt 866261af5d5a2dcf15a3]."
  (source: same file as above)

  (source: `root/Testing Methodologies and Quality Assurance Practices/Software Testing Techniques, Measurement, and Management/SUT Measurements and Fault Classification for Test Planning.md`)
