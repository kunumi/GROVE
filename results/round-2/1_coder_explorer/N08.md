### Question

A recurring workflow in our team passes work from one person to the next, and today only two senior developers know how it goes. I want to write it down so that new developers and automated coding agents follow it the same way every time: what must the write-up state, and in what forms can it be written?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
SWEBOK v4 defines a process as a set of interrelated activities that transform inputs into outputs, and it specifies that a process description must include the required inputs, transforming activities and tasks, outputs produced, controls/constraints, enabling mechanisms (tools, resources), inter-process dependencies, and the rationale for non-trivial decisions — all expressed in a way that is easy to understand and correct. Nine distinct notation forms are recognized for writing such a description, from plain prose to formal graphical languages.

# Guidelines

- **State the purpose and conditions of the workflow.** [SWEBOK excerpt 37856bf26653ea9d0559]." The write-up must name what the workflow achieves and when it applies.
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Engineering Process Definition and Structure/Software Engineering Process Definition.md`)

- **Enumerate all required inputs.** "[SWEBOK excerpt 45a4e97d663329a39c21]." Each step must identify the artifacts or information it needs before it can begin.
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Engineering Process Definition and Structure/Software Process Definition and Components.md`)

- **List every activity and task, distinguishing mandatory from optional.** A process consists of *activities* (cohesive groups of tasks) and *tasks* ("required, recommended, or permissible actions [SWEBOK excerpt 4ab0372db9bcbc9951bf]").
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Engineering Process Definition and Structure/Software Engineering Process Definition.md`)

- **Name all outputs produced.** "[SWEBOK excerpt 0abe0273b7db701637cc]." For a hand-off workflow this is essential: each step's output must be precisely named so the next person or agent knows what they are receiving.
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Engineering Process Definition and Structure/Software Process Definition and Components.md`)

- **Specify controls (directives and constraints).** "[SWEBOK excerpt 14540ad380affbb84ca2]) … associated with the processes." Any approval gates, ordering rules, or quality criteria must be stated explicitly.
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Engineering Process Definition and Structure/Software Process Definition and Components.md`)

- **Identify enabling mechanisms (tools, technologies, resources).** The same passage names "[SWEBOK excerpt 612953409ad28294ea78])" as a required element of a process description.
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Engineering Process Definition and Structure/Software Process Definition and Components.md`)

- **Show inter-step dependencies (the hand-off chain).** "Defining software processes … [SWEBOK excerpt 90a39f34546d1aa96bc2]." The write-up must make every hand-off dependency visible.
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Processes and Life Cycle Management/Software Process Definition and Life Cycle Specification.md`)

- **Record the rationale for non-obvious decisions.** "[SWEBOK excerpt ee0494bd58fb8c3445eb]." Work products should capture "([SWEBOK excerpt 1b8e8f8352f24663d0ca]."
  (source: `root/Software Design Concerns, Documentation, and Modeling/Software Design Documentation and Communication Methods/Recording Software Designs.md`)

- **Write it so it is easy to understand and correct.** "[SWEBOK excerpt e11b7590464e728dc819] … [SWEBOK excerpt c22f8f1158b94cc6f9bd] … [SWEBOK excerpt 9b95324fef99d041e01e]."
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Processes and Life Cycle Management/Software Process Definition and Life Cycle Specification.md`)

- **Choose from nine recognized notation forms:** natural language, textual lists of activities/tasks, data-flow diagrams, state charts, IDEF0, Petri nets, UML activity diagrams, BPMN (with dedicated tooling), or named process formalisms (e.g., RUP). For automated agents in particular, prefer model-based forms because "[SWEBOK excerpt e2f728d486397fa1c2eb] … which might introduce ambiguity and incompleteness."
  (source: `root/Software Engineering Processes and Quality Assurance/…/Software Process Foundations and Measurement/Software Process Infrastructure Tools and Notations.md`; `root/Software Design Concerns, Documentation, and Modeling/Software Design Documentation and Communication Methods/Software Design Notations and Model-Based Approaches.md`)

# Gaps
- The BoK does not give specific guidance on tailoring process documentation for **automated coding agents** beyond the general principle of avoiding ambiguity.
- The BoK does not address knowledge-elicitation strategies for the scenario where only a small number of experts hold tacit knowledge.
- The BoK does not prescribe *which* notation to choose for a given type of workflow; it only enumerates the available options.
