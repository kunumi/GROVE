### Question

What principles should guide the design of how a program signals and deals with abnormal conditions, and what alternatives exist for handling errors?

Answer with a list of guidelines. After each guideline, cite the source it comes from: the path of the SWEBOK file if you have it, otherwise a verbatim quote of at least 8 words from the passage, in the form (source: ...).

### Answer

# Summary
The SWEBOK v4 identifies error handling as a key factor in achieving correctness and robustness, and prescribes a layered approach: use Design by Contract and defensive programming to prevent errors from occurring, apply assertions to detect them at runtime, choose from multiple handling alternatives (neutral values, error codes, logging, shutdown, exceptions), and design exception-handling policies carefully with explicit principles. For higher reliability, fault-tolerance techniques such as retry, voting algorithms, and benign-value substitution provide additional safety nets.

# Guidelines

- **Error handling choices affect correctness and robustness.** The way errors are handled directly affects the [SWEBOK excerpt d2cfcc0342314203cf76]. (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

- **Use assertions to check for errors, especially in high-reliability programs.** Assertions are executable predicates that perform runtime checks; "[SWEBOK excerpt 4b5d09b3a87e6f492d8f]." (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Parameterization, Templates, Generics, and Assertions.md`)

- **Consider multiple error-handling alternatives beyond exceptions.** These include [SWEBOK excerpt fab09da3601a283baf81]. (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

- **Follow the throw/try-catch structure for exception handling.** "[SWEBOK excerpt c20942dd347572e53401]." (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

- **Include all information that led to the exception in the exception message.** This is one of the common principles for carefully designed exception-handling policies. (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

- **Avoid empty catch blocks.** Silently swallowing exceptions is explicitly listed as an anti-pattern to avoid. (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

- **Know what exceptions library code throws.** Callers must understand what third-party or library routines can signal. (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

- **Consider a centralized exception reporter and standardize exception use across the program.** "[SWEBOK excerpt 02e7dd5a67c854de8ec7]." (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Error Handling, Exception Handling, and Fault Tolerance.md`)

- **Use defensive programming to protect routines from invalid inputs.** Check all input parameter values and decide [SWEBOK excerpt 6fde48440859dca72980] to enforce this. (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Design by Contract and Defensive Programming.md`)

- **Apply Design by Contract to clarify what constitutes an error.** Preconditions and postconditions form a contract that makes valid vs. invalid states explicit. (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Contract Design and Error Handling Strategies/Design by Contract and Defensive Programming.md`)

- **Use fault-tolerance strategies when recovery may not be possible.** Back up and retry, use voting algorithms with auxiliary code, or replace erroneous values with phony values that have benign effects. (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Fault Tolerance Techniques and Executable Models.md`)

- **Compile assertions out of production builds.** Assertions are compiled in [SWEBOK excerpt f987e2908349b5a55762]." (source: `root/Software Construction Techniques and Practices/Assertions and Design by Contract in Programming.md`)

# Gaps
- The BoK does not analyze the trade-offs between returning error codes vs. throwing exceptions.
- No guidance on language-specific exception hierarchies (checked vs. unchecked).
- No coverage of modern patterns such as Result types or monadic error propagation.
- No specific guidance on error handling in concurrent or distributed systems beyond the listed fault-tolerance strategies.
